# Methodology — Claim-Coverage Evaluation

**Complete pre-registration rules, thresholds, calculations, findings and validity constraints.**

**Everything in §1–§4 was fixed before any document was collected.** §5 onward is result.

---

# 1. The measure

> **`C_a` = (author-stated claims recoverable from the extract alone) ÷ (total author-stated claims)**

**Recoverable** = a reader who saw only that extract, and nothing else, could state the claim and
defend it.

**Unit of analysis is the claim, not the document.** A 5-claim document contributes 5 rows and a
3-claim document contributes 3, so documents that assert more weigh more — which is the intended
behaviour, since the measure is about content recovery, not about documents.

## Extract size

**Five verbatim sentences per document**, fixed. Not tuned, not varied by document length.

**This is a hard budget and it is meant to be.** A budget that expands with document length would
measure a different thing for every document and could not be compared across a corpus.

---

# 2. Corpus rules, fixed in advance

| Rule | Value | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum documents | **10** | Below this a single document moves the headline by ~10% |
| Minimum distinct sources | **3** | Fewer measures one publisher's house style |
| Per-source cap | **4** | Enforced; the first build violated this and was rebuilt, not excused |
| Claims per document | **3–5** | Fewer is not a claim set; more dilutes toward a summary |
| Minimum body length | **1,500 words** | Shorter documents make 5 sentences trivially sufficient |
| Degenerate claim filter | reject any claim < 20 characters | Guards against list artifacts |

**Realized:** 12 documents · 4 journals · 50 claims · 2,990–14,879 body words · 77 screened.

## What is removed from the body before selection

**Abstract · author highlights · graphical abstract · teaser · figures · tables · formulae ·
methods · declarations · acknowledgements · supplementary material.**

> **Without this the evaluation degenerates into "can the selector find the abstract?", which is a
> different and much easier question.**

---

# 3. Ground truth — the contamination control

**Author-written `author-highlights`: 3–5 discrete claims, composed by each paper's own authors,
published before this evaluation existed.**

**This eliminates rather than manages the dominant validity threat.** If the evaluator writes the
ground truth *and* picks the sentences, coverage approaches 100% by construction and measures the
evaluator's memory.

## Blinding, implemented structurally

1. The builder writes `corpus/body/` and `corpus/truth/` to **separate directories**.
2. Arm 1 selection is performed reading **only** `corpus/body/`.
3. **No truth file is opened until all 60 sentences are locked and verbatim-verified.**
4. `corpus/truth/` is committed and **is never regenerated by any script** — a script that can
   rewrite the answers is a script that can be edited into agreeing with the result.

**Arm 2 needs no blinding**: it is a deterministic function of the body text.

---

# 4. Selection rules, fixed in advance

## Arm 1 — model-guided

> *Select exactly five sentences from the document below. Reproduce each one
> character-for-character as it appears — do not shorten, join, correct, or re-punctuate. Choose the
> five that together carry the most of what the document asserts. Output five lines, nothing else,
> no numbering, no commentary.*

**Applied unchanged to all 12 documents. Zero per-document overrides.** Where the rule produced a
weak selection, that is the result.

## Arm 2 — mechanical, no model

**TextRank-style, Python standard library only, no network.**

- Sentence split with abbreviation and decimal protection
- Eligible sentences: **15–60 words** (fallback 8–90 if fewer than 5 qualify)
- Similarity: content-word set intersection ÷ `log(|a|+1) + log(|b|+1)`, stopword-filtered
- Power iteration, damping **0.85**, **60 iterations**
- Top 5 by score, emitted in document order

**Deterministic. Same input, same output, no seed.**

## Verbatim rule

**Every selected sentence must be locatable in the source body by exact string search.** A failure
permits **one** regeneration for that document and **the failure is counted**, never hidden.

---

# 5. Grading rule

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Binary** | Y or N. **No partial credit** — partial credit is where generous grading enters |
| **Conjunctive claims** | Every part must be present, or **N** |
| **Hesitation** | Resolves to **N**, by rule declared before grading |
| **Scope** | Judged against the extract alone; no outside knowledge, no other document |

**Structural check:** recovered ≤ total, per arm. Asserted in `verify_and_score.py`.

**Direction of strictness, declared:** the grader held a prior favouring the mechanical arm. Strict
grading lowers both arms and therefore **works against that prior**. A laxer reading raises both
roughly proportionally and moves neither band.

**Disputed rows carry a note in `results/grading.csv`** so a re-grader can disagree with the specific
judgment rather than the total.

---

# 6. Thresholds

## As used in the reference run

| `C_a` | Band |
| --- | --- |
| **≥ 70%** | PASS |
| **50–69%** | REVISE |
| **< 50%** | STOP |

## Their provenance, and the warning

**70%** came from one product's bar: *if three claims in ten are missing, the reader must open the
document anyway, so the tool saved nothing.* **50%** was set far above chance — five sentences drawn
at random from a dense document recover very little — as the point below which selection is doing
something but not something useful.

> **Both are product judgments, not properties of the instrument. Re-derive them, in advance, from
> your own tolerance. Inheriting them unexamined is the most likely way to misuse this asset.**

## The unit refusal, carried forward

**ROUGE and other n-gram overlap scores may not be mapped onto these bands.** ROUGE recall is
n-gram overlap against a reference; claim recovery is whether a reader could state and defend an
assertion. They are different quantities and do not correlate reliably at the item level. **Any
invented conversion between them is threshold-weakening wearing a methodology's name.**

## The self-graded-arm cap

**Pre-registered rule, and it is general:** where the grader has a stake in an arm's outcome, that
arm may return a **negative or inconclusive** verdict but **never a passing one**.

**A favourable number from a favourable grader cannot clear a bar. An unfavourable one is
informative.** In the reference run the cap never had to bind — the model arm landed in REVISE on its
own — but it was declared before collection, which is the only time such a rule can legitimately be
declared.

## The mechanical arm's failure mode — the transferable part

**TextRank reliably selects methods boilerplate.**

In document 11 all five selected sentences describe a contract research organization's standard
operating procedures, recovering **zero** of the paper's three claims. Documents 03, 10 and 11
returned nothing at all.

> **TextRank rewards sentences whose vocabulary recurs. In a scientific paper the recurring
> vocabulary is the apparatus, not the finding.**

**This is a property of unsupervised centrality on technical prose, not of these twelve papers.**
Anyone reaching for a cheap extractive selector on methodological writing should expect it.

# 8. Validity constraints

## What weakens the result

| Constraint | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| **Single grader, unblinded, with a declared prior favouring arm 2** | Would inflate arm 2. Countered by strict grading and the §6 cap |
| **n = 12 documents, 50 claims** | Wide interval on the headline; the ~40-point gap between arms is far larger than the noise, the absolute levels are not |
| **One document type** (biomedical and applied-science papers, 4 journals) | Does not generalize to news, legal, narrative or conversational text without re-running |
| **Author highlights are marketing-adjacent** | Authors write highlights to be striking, which may bias them toward findings a body sentence states plainly — this would *raise* coverage, not lower it |
| **Five-sentence budget is arbitrary** | A different budget yields a different number; only same-budget comparisons are meaningful |
| **Arm 1's selector is a large model** | A different model would score differently. Arm 1 is not "the ceiling of extraction" — it is one selector |

## What the design does protect against

**Ground-truth circularity** (external authorship + structural blinding) · **abstract-finding**
(summary sections stripped) · **corpus cherry-picking** (77 screening decisions logged, per-source
cap enforced) · **selector tuning** (fixed rule, zero overrides) · **generous grading** (binary,
conjunctive, hesitation→N) · **grader self-confirmation** (pre-registered cap) ·
**threshold drift** (bands and floors set before collection) · **non-reproducible corpus** (pinned
by identifier, SHA-256 per body).

## What it cannot establish, restated

**Demand · frequency · severity · business impact · willingness to pay · retention · any
individual's preference · faithfulness of generated prose.** **A high score buys the right to run a
test with people in it. It is not that test.**

---

# 9. A companion measure that was designed but never run

**For completeness, and because its absence is itself a finding.**

The originating design also specified a **preference measure**: present, blind and in random order,
*(a)* five verbatim sentences and *(b)* five one-to-one compressions of those same five sentences,
and record which a reader finds more useful. Bar: **≥ 60% preferring (b)** means the generative step
earns its place.

**It was never run, and it could not be substituted from public evidence.** A search of the
human-evaluation literature returned only **2 qualifying studies, 1 with a per-item preference
share** against a pre-registered floor of 3 and 2. **The floor was missed and no verdict was read**,
even though both available data points fell the same side of 60%.

> **The reason is structural, and it is worth knowing before anyone tries again: the published
> literature compares *system summaries*. This measure compares five verbatim sentences against five
> compressions of those same sentences. That contrast is a product decision, not a modelling task,
> and nobody publishes it.**

**Closing it requires blind pairwise judgments from n > 1 people on your own corpus.** The corpus,
the extracts and the grading scaffolding in this directory are exactly what such a test would need.
