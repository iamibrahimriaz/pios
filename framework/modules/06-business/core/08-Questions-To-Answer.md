# Questions To Answer

---

# Overview

This module has more **OPERATOR** questions than any other. Runway, existing access,
appetite for risk and target company shape are facts about the operator's situation, not
about the market — and none can be researched.

It also has more genuinely unknowable inputs than any other module. CAC and churn cannot
be established before launch. Recording them as assumptions is correct; inventing them is
the module's characteristic failure.

---

# 1. The Payer

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Who signs the payment? | No business model |
| 1.2 | Is that the same person who uses it? | The approval path is invisible |
| 1.3 | Which budget does it come from? | The purchase may have no home |
| 1.4 | Do they have authority to spend at this price? | The sale stalls at approval |
| 1.5 | Who else must approve? | The blocker surfaces during the sale |
| 1.6 | What event makes them buy now rather than later? | No trigger; indefinite deferral |
| 1.7 | Have they bought anything comparable before? | Budget precedent unknown |

**Willingness, ability and authority are three questions, not one.**

| Fails on | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Willingness | "Interesting, but we're fine as we are" |
| Ability | "We love it, we can't afford it" |
| Authority | "I'd need to take this to the partners" |

# 2. Value

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | What does the problem cost them per occurrence? | From 04-problem — no value basis |
| 2.2 | How often does it occur annually? | Annual value cannot be computed |
| 2.3 | What share of that value does the product capture? | Value is overstated |
| 2.4 | Is the cost visible to them, or absorbed? | Sales difficulty misjudged |
| 2.5 | Does the payer bear the cost, or someone else? | Willingness misattributed |
| 2.6 | What does the switching cost subtract from that value? | Net value overstated |

> **2.4.** A cost people have normalized — unpaid overtime, a workaround they no longer
> notice — is real and hard to sell against. Name it here; it does not reduce the value,
> it increases the sales effort.

# 3. Pricing

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | What do competitors charge? | From 05 — no market anchor |
| 3.2 | What is the price range in this market? | Positioning has no context |
| 3.3 | What share of delivered value does our price represent? | Price is arbitrary |
| 3.4 | **Why would someone pay when the status quo is free?** | The hardest objection unanswered |
| 3.5 | What pricing model fits how value is delivered? | Model mismatched to usage |
| 3.6 | What is the unit — per user, per organization, per transaction? | Strategic choice unmade |
| 3.7 | Should there be a free tier? What does it cost to serve? | Free users may be unprofitable |
| 3.8 | **OPERATOR** — Any price point you would refuse to go below? | Constraint discovered late |

---

# 4. Cost to Serve

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | What does infrastructure cost per customer per year? | Margin is assumed, not derived |
| 4.2 | What does support cost per customer? | Margin overstated |
| 4.3 | What do third-party services cost per customer? | Hidden variable cost |
| 4.4 | If AI is used, what does it cost per operation, per customer? | This has ended products |
| 4.5 | What ongoing compliance cost does module 02 imply? | Regulated markets carry fixed costs |
| 4.6 | Does cost to serve fall with scale, or stay flat? | Margin trajectory unknown |

> **4.4.** A per-user AI cost that consumes a meaningful share of a per-user price is a
> structural problem, not an optimization. Module 14 will produce this figure; if it runs
> after this module, record the assumption and flag it for revisiting.

# 5. Unit Economics

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | What does it cost to acquire a customer? | LTV:CAC cannot be computed |
| 5.2 | On what basis — a channel, a benchmark, or a guess? | The key input is unqualified |
| 5.3 | What is annual churn, and on what basis? | Lifetime is fabricated |
| 5.4 | What is the gross margin, derived from cost to serve? | Margin assumed |
| 5.5 | What is LTV:CAC, and against what threshold? | No viability test |
| 5.6 | What is the payback period? | Cash requirement unknown |
| 5.7 | **Which single input, wrong by half, changes the verdict?** | The model has no identified weak point |
| 5.8 | What happens if CAC doubles and churn doubles? | No sensitivity; false precision |

# 6. Go To Market

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1 | Who are the first ten customers, specifically? | No route to revenue |
| 6.2 | How would each be reached? | The plan is a channel list |
| 6.3 | Why would they say yes? | The pitch is untested |
| 6.4 | **OPERATOR** — What access, audience or relationships already exist? | CAC is guessed from zero |
| 6.5 | Which channels reach the segment module 03 described? | Channels chosen by fashion |
| 6.6 | What sales motion does the price imply? | Motion mismatched to price |
| 6.7 | How long is the sales cycle? | Runway risk invisible |
| 6.8 | **OPERATOR** — How much runway is available? | Cycle length cannot be judged against it |

> **6.7 and 6.8 together.** A nine-month enterprise sales cycle and twelve months of
> runway is a structural finding that belongs in the risk register, not a scheduling
> detail.

# 8. Viability

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8.1 | Does the model pass its thresholds? | No verdict |
| 8.2 | Which threshold fails first as assumptions worsen? | No early warning |
| 8.3 | What would have to be true for this to work? | Conditions unstated |
| 8.4 | What evidence argues against viability? | The adversarial pass was skipped |
| 8.5 | If the verdict is favorable, which assumption is carrying it? | Fragility hidden |

# 9. Questions the Agent Asks Itself

- Did I pick a price and then find reasons for it?
- How many economics inputs are sourced, and how many did I choose?
- Would this price fit inside this segment's plausible software budget?
- Have I genuinely answered why anyone pays when free exists?
- Is my CAC figure derived from anything, or is it a placeholder that acquired authority?
- Could I name one real organization that would buy this?
- Does my document look more certain than my evidence?

# Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Example | Why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price first | "$49/month feels right" | Value never establishes the range |
| Untagged inputs | A precise LTV from six guesses | False rigor |
| Payer assumed | "The doctor buys it" with no approval path | The sale stalls at a partner meeting |
| Free ignored | Price justified only against paid rivals | The main objection unanswered |
| Assumed margin | "80% gross margin, standard for SaaS" | Cost to serve never derived |
| Channels as GTM | "SEO, content, and partnerships" | No first customer |
| Benchmark as fact | "Churn is 5%, industry standard" | Untagged, unverified, load-bearing |

---

> **Interrogation Principle**
>
> The two numbers this module most depends on — acquisition cost and churn —
> cannot be known before launch.
>
> Say so. A model that names its unknowns can be planned around;
> one that fills them in cannot.
