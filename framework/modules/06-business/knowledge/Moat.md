# Moat

---

# What It Is

Whatever prevents the margin from being competed away.

`05-competition` asked whether the *gap* can be held. This asks the narrower economic question: **can the price
be sustained?** They diverge — a product can hold a niche and still lose its margin to a free alternative.

| Mechanism | Durability | Available to a new product? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Switching cost** — stored data, integration, trained staff | High | Accrues over time; zero on day one |
| **Proprietary data** | High while exclusive | Only if access is contractual |
| **Regulatory approval or certification** | High | Yes, if paid for — and it excludes competitors too |
| **Network or workflow effects** | High | Rarely, and never early |
| **Brand trust in a risk-averse market** | High | No — earned over years |
| **Distribution relationship** | High | Sometimes, through a partner |
| Being cheaper | None | Yes, and it is not defensible |
| Better product | Low | Yes, temporarily |

The first column's top rows all share a property: they accumulate. That is why a new product's honest answer is
usually **none yet**, with a stated path to one.

---

# When It Applies

At Entry 6 (Test), as part of the viability verdict, and carried into `07-strategy` as a condition.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Say plainly if there is no moat.** `05-competition` established the same rule for the gap: not defensible is a
legitimate finding that must be stated. Many good businesses start without one — but it has to be a known
condition, not a discovery after launch.

**Name the moat that would accrue, and what builds it.** Stored history, an integration, a certification, an
exclusive data agreement. Then name what in `08-product` or `10-execution` actually produces it. A moat nobody is
building does not arrive.

**Check the regulatory route seriously.** In regulated markets, certification is a genuine and purchasable moat —
expensive, slow, and exactly as excluding of competitors as it is of you. `02-market` Frame 2 costed it.

**Distinguish switching cost from lock-in.** Value that accumulates for the customer — their history, their
configuration — retains them willingly. Contractual and technical obstruction retains them resentfully, and it
shows up at renewal and in references.

**Reconcile with `05-competition`'s answer.** If module 05 found the gap indefensible and this finds a strong
moat, one of them is wrong.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Technology is claimed as a moat and almost never is.** The same tools are available to everyone, including the
incumbent with the distribution. For AI features this is acute: `05-competition`'s test — what survives if the
model is free tomorrow — is the relevant question.

**First-mover advantage is assumed to be a moat.** It is a head start. It becomes a moat only if something
accumulates during it, and if nothing does, the advantage decays to nothing.

**Difficulty is confused with defensibility.** Hard to build delays a competitor; funding removes the delay. Only
unwillingness — a commercial conflict — actually defends.

**A moat is described that requires scale the model does not project.** Network effects at a thousand customers
are irrelevant to a plan reaching thirty. State the threshold at which the mechanism starts working.

**The absence of a moat is smoothed over rather than stated.** That omission removes the single most important
condition `07-strategy` needs when deciding whether to sequence for speed.
