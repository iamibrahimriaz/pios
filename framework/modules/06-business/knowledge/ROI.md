# ROI

---

# What It Is

What the problem costs the payer today, and what share of that this product removes — established **before** a
price is considered.

```
annual value at stake = cost per occurrence × occurrences per year
capturable value      = annual value at stake × share the product actually removes
```

Both figures come from `04-problem`: cost per occurrence and frequency, already tagged. A product that removes
half a problem delivers half the value, not all of it.

Two qualifications decide how sellable that value is:

| | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Is the value visible?** | An absorbed cost — unpaid overtime, a normalized workaround, a role that exists to bridge a gap — is real but not felt, and far harder to sell against than an invoiced cost of the same size |
| **Does the payer bear it?** | Value felt by the user may not translate into willingness by the buyer |

---

# When It Applies

In Entry 2 (Value), before Entry 3 (Price). The order is the module's central discipline: **a price chosen first
and justified afterward is a preference wearing a business model.**

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Carry `04-problem`'s figures through unchanged, with their tags.** If the cost per occurrence was
`[assumption]`, the value at stake is an assumption and the price built on it inherits that standing.

**State the capture share explicitly and defend it.** Removing 40% of a problem is a normal, honest outcome.
Assuming full capture is the standard inflation, and it doubles or triples the value case invisibly.

**Mark absorbed costs as absorbed.** If module 04 flagged the cost as absorbed, carry that forward — it changes
the sales difficulty, not the value. Unpaid evenings are real value to the person and produce no line item for
the buyer to reclaim.

**Convert to the payer's unit.** Clinician hours matter to the clinician; the practice cares about sessions,
claims, or locum costs. The ROI case has to be denominated in something the payer's own budget recognizes.

**Note where value is risk rather than cash.** Avoided penalties, avoided incidents and reduced audit exposure
are legitimate value with a probability attached. State the probability and the consequence separately rather
than blending them into a single figure.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Saved time is counted as saved money and usually is not.** An hour recovered from an unpaid evening becomes an
evening. Salaried time saved is real value to the person and frequently no cash saving to the employer — and the
employer is usually the payer.

**Full capture is assumed silently.** The arithmetic is presented as the problem's total cost, as if the product
eliminated it entirely. The share is the single most consequential unstated input in the calculation.

**ROI is computed for the user and presented to the buyer.** Where they differ, this produces a case that is
true and unpersuasive — the strongest version of the module's payer failure.

**A large value at stake is treated as willingness to pay.** People decline to spend money solving expensive
problems constantly, particularly absorbed ones. Value sets the ceiling on price; it does not create demand.

**Precision creeps in.** A value case built on one estimated minute count should be stated to one significant
figure. It will be quoted in `07-strategy` and the executive summary with whatever precision it was given.
