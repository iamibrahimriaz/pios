# Discounts

---

# What It Is

The gap between the list price and the price actually collected — and the rule that every reduction buys
something.

| Discount | What it should buy |
| --- | --- |
| **Annual prepayment** | Cash and a twelve-month commitment |
| **Multi-year** | Commitment, at a real cost to future pricing freedom |
| **Design partner or early adopter** | Feedback, a reference, and a case study — named in the agreement |
| **Volume** | Lower cost to serve per unit, actually realized |
| **"To close the deal"** | Nothing. This is the one to name and avoid |

Every downstream figure must use the **effective** price. LTV, revenue, payback and the value ratio computed on
list price overstate by exactly the discount rate, and that error is invisible in the arithmetic.

---

# When It Applies

In Entry 3 (Price) as a policy, and in Entry 4 (Economics) as the figure the model actually uses.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**State an expected discount rate and model the effective price.** For any product with a human sales motion,
assume discounting occurs. A model on list price is a model of a business nobody runs.

**Write down what each discount requires in return**, and require it in the agreement. A reference or a case study
promised informally is not received.

**Set a floor and say what it protects.** The floor is the price below which margin, payback, or the value
positioning breaks. `Financial-Risks.md` identifies which threshold fails first, and that is the floor's
justification.

**Prefer a shorter term or narrower scope to a lower price.** Reducing what is delivered preserves the price
anchor; reducing the price resets it permanently for that customer and for anyone they talk to.

**Decide the early-customer policy in advance.** Design-partner pricing is legitimate and should be explicitly
time-boxed, with the transition to standard pricing agreed at the start rather than negotiated later.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Discounting is planned informally and never modeled**, so every projection is optimistic by an unstated margin.
This is the quietest arithmetic error in the module because nothing in the model looks wrong.

**A discount is treated as a concession rather than a trade.** Given for nothing, it teaches the buyer the price
was invented — which invites a further request at renewal.

**Early discounts are assumed to be temporary and rarely are.** The first customers become reference customers, and
raising their price later is the hardest commercial conversation available. Assume the early price is the price
unless the agreement says otherwise.

**Discounting is used to answer the free incumbent.** It cannot be — zero is the floor, and moving toward it
concedes the value argument. `Free.md` covers what the real answer looks like.

**Public discount patterns undermine the list price.** A permanently discounted list price is a list price nobody
believes, and it makes the value anchor from Entry 2 unusable in a negotiation.
