# Reflection

---

# Overview

This module is where a plan meets a person's calendar. These questions are about whose
calendar it is.

---

# On the Commitments

**Did you agree to these, or write them?** If you are the operator, those are the same
act — which is precisely why it is worth separating them deliberately.

**What did you promise because it sounded professional?** Out-of-hours coverage is the
usual one.

**Have you told the customer the honest version?** A limitation stated at the sale is a
term. The same limitation discovered during an incident is a failure.

---

# On the Cost

**Whose time is free in this model?** Yours, usually.

**What is the dominant line?** If it surprised you, that is the module working.

**Would you take this work at that rate for someone else?** A useful test of whether the
rate you used is real.

---

# On the Breach

**Did the ceiling hold?** If it did comfortably, check that human time was priced.

**If it did not, what did you do?** Regress or absorb. Be honest about which, because the
absorption always presents as a refinement.

**Who makes that decision?** Naming them matters. In a solo operation the answer is you,
and the value of writing it down is that it becomes a decision rather than a drift.

---

# On the Runbooks

**Which one have you actually tested?** Restore is the one everyone states and nobody
rehearses, and the first real attempt is where the incomplete backup is discovered.

**Which one would you rather not need at 3am?** That is the one to hand to a stranger
first.

**What is the tempting action in your system?** If you have not named it, it is available
to whoever is on call, including you at 3am.

---

# On the Obligations

**How many have your name on them?** Count. Then add the cadences and see what it comes
to per month.

**Is that sustainable, honestly, for how long?** "For a defined period, not indefinitely"
is an acceptable answer and a much better one than silence.

**What happens to them when you are unwell?** Not a hypothetical. Plan it now, while it
is a paragraph rather than a crisis.

---

# On the Support Queue

**Which recurring request is a design defect?** Most of the top three, usually.

**Which burden is correct and permanent?** Those need pricing, not fixing — and module 06
needs to know.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your cost models never include your hours | The dominant cost is invisible to you |
| Your runbooks read like notes to yourself | They will fail with anyone else holding them |
| Your obligations have roles rather than names | You are avoiding assignment, including your own |
| You have never regressed a plan | Breaches are being absorbed |
| Your coverage tables exceed your headcount | You are describing capability you do not have |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> If this product succeeds, what does your Tuesday look like in eighteen months?

Add up the cadences, the support hours, the delivery time and the on-call. For many
small products the answer is a week that cannot be worked, and discovering that now
changes the price, the scope or the plan — while all three are still cheap to change.

Success is a load. This is the only module that asks what it weighs.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> Everything in this module eventually becomes somebody's week.
>
> The reflection that matters is not whether the plan is complete. It is whether the
> person named in it can carry it, and for how long.
