# Future Improvements

---

# Known Limitations

**Nothing enforces the regress.** A ceiling breach must return to module 06 or 07. The
module can report the breach, recommend a response, and continue — and every gate still
passes. This is the same unenforced-chain problem that affects the declared shortfall,
and it sits at the framework's most consequential arithmetic.

**Human time has no required line.** The most commonly omitted and most frequently
dominant cost is carried by guidance.

**The 3am test cannot be automated.** It is the module's best standard and it requires a
person and a runbook. Nothing approximates it in a gate.

**Sustainability is unmeasured.** The plan can assign eleven recurring obligations to one
name and pass. The framework surfaces the inputs and never adds them up.

**Runbook coverage is not checked against failure modes.** Every runbook should correspond
to a module 09 failure mode, and every failure mode should have one. The correspondence is
a convention, so a missing runbook is invisible.

**Alert hygiene is guidance.** Three components per alert, delete what has no action.
Nothing verifies that any alert has a runbook.

**No incident learning loop.** Reviews are required to produce an output — a runbook
change, an alert change, or a roadmap item — and there is no mechanism carrying those
back into the framework's documents.

**Cost is a launch-scale snapshot.** Support and compliance load change shape with volume,
and the model has one column.

---

# Candidate Improvements

| Candidate | What it would fix | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A regress-confirmed state on a breach | The framework's most important check stops being absorbable | Moderate; needs engine state |
| A required `human_time` line | The dominant cost stops being optional | Small, and overdue |
| A runbook-to-failure-mode correspondence check | Missing runbooks become visible | Small |
| An obligation load summary per named owner | Unsustainable plans become visible at a glance | Small, and possibly the most humane change here |
| Alert completeness validation | Alerts without runbooks stop passing | Small |
| Cost at two scales rather than one | The shape change becomes visible | Small |

The fourth row deserves a note. Every input needed to compute one person's monthly
obligation load already exists in the plan — cadences and owners. Summing them is
trivial and nothing does it, which is why unsustainable operations plans read as
thorough ones.

---

# What Should Not Change

**The 3am test stays the runbook standard.** Every softer standard produces documents
that work for their author.

**Commitments stay separated from forecasts.** Mixing them is how a product acquires
obligations nobody agreed to.

**The obligation's four fields stay mandatory.** Cadence, owner, evidence, location.
Dropping evidence is the usual proposal and it is the field that converts compliance from
a claim into a practice.

**The cost check stays cumulative and last.** It is the only place the whole cost is
visible, and moving any component earlier would produce three partial checks that each
pass.

**The rota question stays answerable honestly.** A framework that made an unstaffable
coverage table acceptable would be producing documents rather than plans.
