# Business Model

---

# What It Is

How each competitor makes money — which is the best available predictor of what they will do next, and the most
common source of a durable gap.

| Model | What it makes them unable to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Per seat** | Ship anything that reduces the number of people needed |
| **Per transaction or usage** | Ship efficiency that reduces volume |
| **Services-led** | Ship automation that removes the billable work |
| **Enterprise contracts** | Serve small customers profitably — the sales cost exceeds the revenue |
| **Free, ad or data funded** | Offer the privacy guarantee a regulated buyer requires |
| **Venture-funded, pre-revenue** | Stay in a small segment; growth expectations force them upward |
| **Owner-operated, profitable** | Chase a segment that would not pay back |

Each row is a **structural weakness** — the strongest kind, because closing it would cost them revenue they depend
on. This is where `Gap-Analysis.md` finds most of its defensible answers.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 4 (Price) and Move 5 (Locate). It is inferred from pricing, packaging and hiring rather than from any
statement a competitor makes.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Infer the model from the pricing page and the job postings.** Per-seat pricing, minimum contract sizes, an
implementation fee, a large sales team, a professional-services line — each one tells you where the revenue comes
from and therefore what it defends.

**Follow the money for free products.** A free tool is funded by a paid tier, an adjacent business, data, or investor
patience. Each implies a different future, and in regulated markets the data-funded case is often disqualifying for
the buyer.

**Note the sales motion, because it constrains the segment.** A competitor requiring a six-figure contract and a
three-month procurement cycle cannot serve single-handed practices at any price. That is the "different sales motion"
row in the defensibility table, and it is durable.

**Check whether venture funding forces them upmarket.** A funded competitor in the target segment today may be
obliged to leave it, which is an opening arriving rather than closing.

**Hand the finding to `06-business`.** A competitor's model constrains the pricing this product can adopt, and it
also reveals which models the market has already accepted.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Business models are inferred and then stated as fact.** The inference is usually sound and should still carry an
`[inferred: basis]` tag — the basis being the pricing page, the hiring, or the packaging.

**A commercial conflict is assumed to be permanent.** Competitors do occasionally cannibalize themselves, usually
when threatened credibly. It buys years, not immunity.

**Their model is treated as the market's model.** That a market is sold per seat means it was sold that way, not
that it must be. A different model is sometimes the differentiation itself — and `06-business` is where that is
decided.

**Funding status is read as strength rather than as constraint.** A funded competitor has resources and obligations,
and the obligations are more predictive of behavior.

**Non-commercial competitors are omitted.** Open-source tools, internally built systems and government-provided
software have no revenue model to conflict with, which makes them unusually persistent — and free.
