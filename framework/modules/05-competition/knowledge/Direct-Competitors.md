# Direct Competitors

---

# What It Is

A product solving the **same problem** for the **same segment**. Both halves are required — a product solving the
same problem for a different segment is indirect, and the distinction changes how it is displaced.

| Type | Solves | For | Displaced by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | The same problem | The same segment | Being better |
| Indirect | The same or adjacent problem | A different segment | Being appropriate to this segment |
| Status quo | Partially, through habit and effort | Everyone | Being worth the disruption |

"Being better" is the lowest bar in that column, which is why direct competitors are the least dangerous
category and the one that receives most of the attention.

# When It Applies

In Move 2 (Classify), then scored in Move 3 (Test) against the ranked problems.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Verify the segment, not just the problem.** A product addressing the same problem for enterprises is not a
direct competitor to a tool for single-handed practices, whatever the feature lists suggest. `Pricing-Comparison.md`
usually settles it faster than any feature table.

**Read the changelog and the support forum, not the homepage.** What has shipped and what breaks are facts. The
marketing site is a statement of ambition.

**Establish how entrenched they are.** Number of customers in the target segment, contract length, and depth of
integration. An entrenched competitor with a mediocre product is harder to displace than a good product with no
installed base.

**Note what they are structurally unable to do.** A competitor's commercial model, existing customer commitments
and architecture constrain them, and those constraints are what `Gap-Analysis.md` tests for defensibility. This is
the most valuable observation available about a direct competitor.

**Score them honestly in Move 3.** If a competitor solves the top-ranked problem well, write "well". A matrix
where the incumbent solves nothing well is an advocacy document, and `07-strategy` will make a bad decision on it.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Direct competitors absorb attention out of proportion to their threat.** They are findable, describable and
comparable — so the analysis concentrates on them while the status quo, which holds most of the market, gets a
single row.

**Feature parity is mistaken for competitiveness.** Matching a direct competitor's feature list produces a product
with no reason to be chosen. `Feature-Comparison.md` covers why table stakes and differentiation are different
columns.

**Their weaknesses are assumed to be permanent.** A competitor's current gap may be a roadmap item. The question
`Gap-Analysis.md` asks — what stops them shipping this in six months — applies specifically here.

**Being newer is treated as an advantage.** A modern stack is not a customer benefit, and `Technology-Comparison.md`
says why. The incumbent's advantage is distribution and trust, neither of which is affected by their architecture.

**Small competitors get dismissed.** A niche product with deep fit in the chosen segment is a harder obstacle than
a large generalist, because it is competing on exactly the ground being contested.

> **Concept Note**
>
> Direct competitors are displaced by being better — the easiest bar in
> the market and the one everyone aims at.

> Their real significance is what their business model forbids them
> from doing.
