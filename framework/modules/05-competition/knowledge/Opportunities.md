# Opportunities

---

# What It Is

The openings the analysis produced, expressed finally as one sentence:

> For «segment» who «need», «product» is a «category» that «benefit», unlike «alternative», which «limitation».

> **The test: could the leading competitor make the same claim?**
>
> If yes, it is not positioning — it is a description of the category.

The module also requires a statement of which contest is being entered:

| | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| **Existing category** | Competing on comparison — buyers already know what to compare |
| **New category** | Paying for education — buyers must first understand why this exists |

Both are valid. The cost differs, and `11-growth` needs to know which one it is funding.

# When It Applies

In Move 6 (Position), last in the module, drawing on the coverage table and the defensibility answer.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Build the claim from a row in the coverage table.** The "unlike" clause names a real competitor and a real
limitation that was scored, not a generic weakness. That is what makes the sentence unavailable to anyone else.

**Run the test literally.** Write the competitor's name in front of the sentence and read it. "The easiest way to
manage your practice" survives that substitution, which is why it is not positioning.

**Say whether the category exists, and cost it.** Category creation is expensive in `11-growth` — every acquisition
conversation starts with education, and CAC reflects it. Entering an existing category is cheaper and puts you on a
comparison grid you do not control.

**Keep it to the one segment.** A positioning statement covering two segments serves neither, and the framework
already required a single prioritized segment in `03-user`.

**Carry the defensibility finding alongside.** An opportunity with no moat is still an opportunity, and stating it
as such is what lets `07-strategy` sequence for speed rather than for durability.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Positioning gets written as aspiration rather than differentiation.** Adjectives — easiest, fastest, most
intuitive — are the standard filling, and they are precisely the claims every competitor also makes. `08-product`
bans the same words in requirements for the same reason.

**An opportunity list replaces the single choice.** Five opportunities is a refusal to position. The sentence has
one segment, one need and one contrast because a position is a commitment.

**Category creation is chosen without pricing it.** Being "the first X" sounds like an advantage and is a bill
payable in `11-growth`. New categories are won by companies that could afford to teach a market.

**The limitation clause names something the competitor will fix.** If the "unlike" rests on a roadmap item rather
than a structural constraint, the positioning has a short expiry. `Gap-Analysis.md` established which it is.

**Positioning drifts from the evidence upward.** Each rewrite makes it more quotable and less specific, until it
describes the category. The coverage table is the anchor; if the sentence no longer traces to a row in it, it has
become marketing.
