# Marketing Comparison

---

# What It Is

An assessment of how competitors reach and persuade buyers — read as **evidence about the market**, not as a set of
claims to rebut.

| What to read it for | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Who the copy addresses | Which segment they actually sell to — often narrower than the product allows |
| Which channel they invest in | Where this market's buyers can be reached, proven by someone spending money on it |
| Whether there is a sales team | Whether the deal size supports human selling |
| What they lead with | Which problem the market responds to |
| What they never mention | The objection they cannot answer |
| The vocabulary they use | The category name `02-market` was looking for |

Competitors have already paid to learn which channels work in this market. That is the most valuable thing on their
website, and `11-growth` needs it.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 6 (Position), and as channel evidence carried forward to `11-growth`, where every channel claim must cite
where the segment was observed.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Identify the channel each competitor relies on.** Search, conferences, professional bodies, resellers, direct sales,
word of mouth within a profession. Sustained investment in a channel is evidence it converts — the strongest channel
evidence available before running anything.

**Read the audience from the copy, not the feature list.** Language, pricing, and the case studies chosen reveal the
real buyer. A product whose site addresses IT directors is not sold to clinicians, whatever it does.

**Note what they lead with.** The first line of a mature product's homepage is usually the message that tested best.
That is the market telling you which problem it responds to.

**Record the objections their copy pre-empts.** A prominent security page, a migration guarantee, a compliance
section — each one names an objection this product will also face, discovered for free.

**Use their vocabulary to check the category.** If every competitor uses a term, that is the category name. If none
does, `Opportunities.md`'s new-category cost applies.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Marketing describes intent, and gets recorded as capability.** A claimed feature may be beta, deprecated, or
aspirational. `Feature-Comparison.md` requires the score to come from what the product does, evidenced in changelogs,
reviews and forums.

**Loud marketing is read as market share.** Visibility and installed base are different, and in institutional markets
the quiet incumbent frequently holds the accounts. Spend is not penetration.

**Their positioning gets used as the frame to argue against.** Building a position as the negation of a competitor's
claim concedes the terms of the comparison. `Opportunities.md` requires a claim traceable to a coverage row instead.

**The absence of marketing is read as an absence of competition.** The status quo does no marketing at all and holds
most of the market. Internally built systems and professional habits have no website.

**Channel evidence is copied without the segment.** A channel working for a competitor selling to large trusts says
nothing about reaching single-handed practices. `11-growth` requires the channel and the segment together.
