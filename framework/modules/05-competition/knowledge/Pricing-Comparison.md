# Pricing Comparison

---

# What It Is

The recorded price of every competitor — captured with its source and date, or marked explicitly unavailable.

> Never estimate silently. "Around $50/month" with no source imports a guess into `06-business`'s revenue model,
> where it becomes a business case.

| Situation | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Public pricing page | The figure, what is included, **and the date accessed** |
| Enterprise "contact us" | Mark unavailable — and note the signal: it implies a sales motion and a price floor |
| Free product | How it is funded; that determines what it will do next |
| Usage-based | The unit, and the cost at a realistic volume rather than the headline rate |

Then read what pricing reveals. A product at ten times the price of another is not competing for the same buyer,
whatever its feature list says. **The price tells you who it was built for** — often more reliably than the
marketing does.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 4 (Price), after coverage is scored. It feeds `06-business` directly, which is why the sourcing discipline
is strict.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Date every figure.** Prices change and pages get rewritten. An undated price is unverifiable six weeks later, and
`06-business` will still be using it.

**Record what the price includes, not just the number.** Seats, usage caps, support tier, contract length, onboarding
fees. Two products at the same headline price can differ by a factor of three in what a buyer actually pays.

**Cost the realistic volume for usage-based pricing.** The headline per-unit rate is designed to look small. Multiply
it by the frequency `03-user` observed.

**Mark "contact us" as unavailable and read the signal.** It means a sales-led motion, a floor below which they will
not go, and a buyer with a procurement process — three facts about the market that a number would not have told you.

**Price the status quo at zero, with its switching cost beside it.** That pairing is the most useful line in the
table: the incumbent is free, and leaving it costs whatever `03-user` calculated.

---

# Where It Misleads

**A silent estimate is the module's most consequential error**, because it does not stay in this module. It becomes
a revenue model, then a projection, then a plan — and by then nobody can see it was invented.

**Public prices are not transaction prices.** Discounting, annual commitments and negotiated enterprise terms mean
the list price is an upper bound. In institutional markets the gap is large and systematic.

**Competitor pricing gets used as the price anchor.** It is one input; the value case from `04-problem`'s cost figure
is the other, and it is usually the stronger of the two. Pricing off competitors alone concedes the position.

**Free is read as a price of zero rather than as a funding model.** A free product is funded by something — a paid
tier, data, an adjacent business, or investor patience — and that funding predicts its behavior. `Business-Model.md`
covers the consequence.

**Cheapest is treated as a strategy.** It is the least defensible position available, particularly against an
incumbent who can absorb a price cut and a status quo already at zero.
