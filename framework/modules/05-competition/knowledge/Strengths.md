# Strengths

---

# What It Is

What each competitor actually has going for it — assessed on the assets that decide markets rather than the ones
that are easiest to compare.

The ranking is consistently counter-intuitive:

| Strength | Durability |
| --- | --- |
| **Installed base and switching cost** | Very high — the account is held before the contest starts |
| **Distribution and relationships** | Very high — access is harder to build than product |
| **Proprietary data** | High while exclusive |
| **Brand trust in a risk-averse market** | High — decisive in regulated and institutional buying |
| **Integration depth** | High — removal is a project, not a decision |
| Feature breadth | Low — matchable |
| Technology and stack | Very low — available to everyone |

The bottom two rows are where competitive analysis usually concentrates, and they are the two that matter least.

---

# When It Applies

Alongside Move 3 (Test), and again in Move 5 (Locate) — a competitor's strengths determine whether a gap can be
held.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Assess distribution before product.** Who already has the relationship, the procurement approval, the integration,
the trust. In institutional markets this decides most contests, and no feature overcomes it directly.

**Be honest where a competitor is genuinely better.** A coverage table showing this product superior on every
ranked problem is an advocacy document, and `07-strategy` will draw the wrong line from it.

**Require evidence for any claimed strength of our own.** This is the rule `02-market`'s SWOT file points at:
"strong AI capability", "fast to iterate", "domain expertise" are self-descriptions until compared against a named
competitor who lacks them. Before a product exists, most claimed strengths are intentions.

**Look for the strength that is invisible.** Nobody advertises "already installed", "no procurement needed", "the
staff already know it". They are the status quo's entire advantage and they do not appear on any comparison grid.

**Note which strengths constrain them.** A large installed base is also an obligation — it makes changes expensive
and slow. That is the mechanism `Weaknesses.md` and `Gap-Analysis.md` exploit.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Strengths get read off marketing sites**, which describe intent and positioning. Capability lives in changelogs,
support forums and reviews.

**Recency is mistaken for advantage.** A newer product with a modern stack has less code and less trust. The second
matters more to a buyer, and `Technology-Comparison.md` explains why the first is not a customer benefit at all.

**Funding is treated as strength.** It buys time and hiring, not customers. A well-funded product with no
distribution is a competitor with a deadline.

**Our own strengths are graded generously and theirs conservatively** — the standard asymmetry, and the reason the
gate requires the coverage table to precede the positioning claim.

**Team strength is claimed without a comparison.** Every team believes it executes well. It is only a competitive
strength if it produces something a competitor demonstrably cannot, and that is a statement about output rather than
about the team.
