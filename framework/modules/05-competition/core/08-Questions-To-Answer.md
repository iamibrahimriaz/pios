# Questions To Answer

---

# Overview

Competition is the most researchable subject in the framework. Pricing, marketing, reviews
and changelogs are public.

Almost nothing here needs the operator, and nothing needs a user. If a question in this
module is being deferred, the likely cause is a narrow search rather than an unknowable
fact.

---

# 1. Enumeration

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Who sells a product solving this problem for this segment? | No direct competitors |
| 1.2 | Who solves it for a different segment? | Indirect entrants invisible |
| 1.3 | Who solves it by a different mechanism — a service, an agency, a person? | Substitutes missed |
| 1.4 | **What do they do today, from `current_workflow`?** | The strongest competitor is missing |
| 1.5 | **How many have this problem and use nothing?** | The real barrier may be inertia |
| 1.6 | What appears on "alternatives to X" pages? | The comparison set buyers actually use |
| 1.7 | What tools are named in job postings for this role? | What organizations actually run |
| 1.8 | What do forum users recommend to each other? | Substitutes and homemade solutions |

> **1.4 and 1.5 are mandatory.** A competitor list containing only funded products has
> missed the two that beat most new entrants: what people already do, and doing nothing.

---

# 2. Classification

| # | Question | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Does this solve the same problem, or an adjacent one? | Direct vs indirect |
| 2.2 | Does it serve the same segment? | Determines whether they meet in a sale |
| 2.3 | How would a buyer displace it — by being better, or by being worth the disruption? | Different bars entirely |
| 2.4 | Is this a product, a habit, or a person? | Substitutes are displaced differently |

---

# 3. Problem Coverage

Ask per competitor, per ranked problem.

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Does this competitor solve P«n» well, partly, or not at all? | The central table is empty |
| 3.2 | Would the user agree it is solved, or only the vendor? | Vendor claims taken at face value |
| 3.3 | Who solves P1 best today, including the status quo? | The real benchmark is unknown |
| 3.4 | Which ranked problems does nobody solve well? | The opening is unlocated |
| 3.5 | Where a competitor solves it partly, what is the residual pain? | The wedge is undefined |

---

# 4. Features

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | What must exist for a product to be taken seriously here? | Table stakes unknown |
| 4.2 | What does each competitor claim as its differentiator? | Their positioning unknown |
| 4.3 | Do reviews suggest they deliver on that claim? | Marketing mistaken for capability |
| 4.4 | What has a competitor shipped repeatedly across releases? | A problem they cannot solve structurally |

<!-- 4.4 is one of the most useful questions available. Repeated fixes in a changelog
     mark a structural weakness, not a temporary bug. -->

---

# 5. Pricing

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | What is the published price, and what is included? | Module 06 has no anchor |
| 5.2 | What pricing model — subscription, usage, license, free? | Revenue shape unknown |
| 5.3 | If pricing is not published, what does that imply? | A signal is missed |
| 5.4 | Who is each price point built for? | Segment fit misread |
| 5.5 | If a competitor is free, how is it funded? | Their next move is unpredictable |
| 5.6 | What is the price range across the market? | Our price has no context |

> **5.3.** "Contact us" pricing implies a sales motion, a price floor, and a buyer who
> expects negotiation. That is information, not an absence of it.

---

# 6. Strengths and Weaknesses

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1 | What is this competitor genuinely best at? | Underestimating them |
| 6.2 | Where do reviews consistently say they fail? | The weakness is anecdotal |
| 6.3 | **Why** are they weak there — structurally? | Weakness may be temporary |
| 6.4 | Would they fix it if attacked? | Defensibility unassessed |
| 6.5 | What is their moat — distribution, data, switching cost, brand? | Their durability unknown |
| 6.6 | Who is their buyer, and is it ours? | May not actually compete |

---

# 7. The Gap

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7.1 | Which ranked problem is least well served? | No opening identified |
| 7.2 | For which segment specifically? | The gap is vague |
| 7.3 | Why does the gap exist? | May be imaginary |
| 7.4 | Why have incumbents not closed it? | The structural reason is the finding |
| 7.5 | **If we succeed, what stops them closing it in six months?** | Defensibility unknown |
| 7.6 | If nothing stops them, what do we have that they cannot copy quickly? | The moat is undefined |
| 7.7 | Is this a feature, or a company? | The whole strategy depends on the answer |

> **7.5 is the most important question in this module.** Most competitive analysis stops
> at 7.3.

---

# 8. Positioning

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8.1 | Who is this for, and what do they need? | No positioning |
| 8.2 | What category does a buyer file this under? | Buyers cannot place it |
| 8.3 | What is the primary alternative, and its limitation? | No contrast |
| 8.4 | **Could the leading competitor make the same claim?** | Not positioning — just category description |
| 8.5 | Existing category, or new one? | The education cost is unbudgeted |

---

# 9. Threats

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9.1 | What if an incumbent ships this as a feature? | Most likely threat unplanned |
| 9.2 | What if the status quo remains good enough? | The commonest failure mode |
| 9.3 | Is anyone newly funded in this space? | Competitive pressure invisible |
| 9.4 | Is the market consolidating? | Entry window may be closing |
| 9.5 | What would we see first if a threat were materializing? | No early warning |

---

# 10. The Graveyard

| # | Question | If unanswered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10.1 | Who tried this and stopped? | A paid-for lesson is ignored |
| 10.2 | Why did they stop? | The structural reason is missed |
| 10.3 | Was it execution, timing, or the market? | Wrong lesson learned |
| 10.4 | Is that reason still true? | May be entering the same trap |

---

# 11. Questions the Agent Asks Itself

- Did I find the real competitors, or the easily findable ones?
- Did I take the status quo seriously, or list it and move on?
- Did I consider people who use nothing at all?
- Did I compare on problems, or fall back on a feature checklist?
- Did I estimate a price without saying so?
- Did I ask what stops an incumbent closing this gap?
- Could a competitor put my positioning statement on their homepage?
- Is this market already well served, and am I avoiding writing that?

---

# Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Example | Why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct competitors only | Three funded products, nothing else | Misses what actually wins |
| Status quo as a formality | Listed, not scored | Understates the hardest competitor |
| Feature checklist as strategy | A large tick table | Everyone ships everything eventually |
| Vendor claims as capability | "They have AI-powered X" | Marketing is not evidence |
| Silent price estimate | "Around $50/month" | A guess becomes a revenue model |
| Gap without defensibility | Opening found, never tested | Builds a feature, not a company |
| Positioning by superlative | "The easiest way to…" | Anyone can claim it |

---

> **Interrogation Principle**
>
> Everything about a competitor is public except the one thing that matters:
> whether they could close your gap if they wanted to.
>
> That question has to be reasoned, and it must not be skipped.
