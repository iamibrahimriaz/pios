# Purpose

---

# Overview

Module 04 established which problems are real.

This module establishes whether anyone already solves them, how well, and — the question
that actually matters — whether there is an opening that could be **held**.

It is the last research module. After it, the run stops describing the world and starts
committing to a direction.

---

# Purpose Statement

> Map who already solves this problem, find where they fail the chosen segment,
> and test whether that opening could survive an incumbent noticing it.

---

# Why This Module Exists

Two failures happen here, and both are common.

**The first is missing the competitor that matters.** A category search returns funded
software products. It does not return the spreadsheet, the assistant, the habit, or the
decision to live with the problem. Those are what most new products actually lose to, and
they are invisible unless deliberately sought.

**The second is finding a gap without asking whether it can be held.** An opening that an
incumbent could close in a quarter is a feature, not a company. Most competitive analysis
stops at "nobody does this well" and never asks "and what happens when they decide to?"

---

# The Distinctive Method

Competitors are compared against the **ranked problems from module 04**, not against a
feature checklist.

Feature tables look thorough and decide nothing. Everyone ships everything eventually; a
capability list tells you what the market considers table stakes, not where you could win.

A problem-coverage table answers a different and more useful question: *for the person we
chose to serve, which of their real problems does nobody solve well?*

---

# Core Objectives

- Enumerate everyone who competes, including what is not a product.
- Score each against the ranked problems, using user evidence rather than vendor claims.
- Capture pricing honestly, or mark it unavailable.
- Locate the gap, and map it to a specific ranked problem.
- Test whether the gap is defensible, and say so plainly when it is not.
- Write a positioning statement a competitor could not also claim.

---

# What AI Should Learn Here

- The status quo is a competitor, and usually the strongest: free, installed, trusted,
  requiring no change and carrying no blame.
- People who have the problem and use nothing are a competitive category.
- Vendor marketing describes intent; reviews and changelogs describe capability.
- A gap with no structural reason for existing is usually not a gap.
- "Not defensible" is a legitimate finding that must be stated, not avoided.
- A price without a source becomes a business model two modules later.

# Scope

**This module covers**

- Competitor enumeration and classification
- Problem coverage scoring
- Table stakes identification
- Pricing capture
- Gap analysis and defensibility
- Positioning
- Threats and the graveyard

**This module does not cover**

| Not here | Belongs to |
| --- | --- |
| Market sizing and boundary | `02-market` |
| Segment and persona | `03-user` |
| Problem ranking | `04-problem` |
| Our own pricing | `06-business` |
| What we build | `07-strategy`, `08-product` |

This module establishes what exists and where the opening is. Module 07 decides what to
do about it.

The **defensibility verdict** is the most consequential single output. A strategy built on
an undefensible gap has a six-month clock attached, and module 07 must be able to see it.

---

# Success Criteria

- The competitor list contains things that are not products.
- The status quo is described with genuine respect for why it persists.
- Competitors are judged on problems, using user evidence.
- Every price is sourced and dated, or honestly marked unavailable.
- The gap maps to a specific ranked problem for a specific segment.
- The six-month question is answered plainly, including when unwelcome.
- The positioning statement could not appear on a competitor's homepage.

---

# Self Assessment

- Did I look for competitors that are not companies?
- Did I judge capability by what users report, or what vendors claim?
- Does my gap correspond to a problem someone actually has?
- Do I know what happens if an incumbent decides to close it?
- Would my positioning survive being read next to a competitor's?
