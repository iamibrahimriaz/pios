# Innovation

---

# What It Is

Doing something differently from how it is currently done — assessed by where the difference sits, because novelty is
priced very differently depending on the layer.

| Novel in | Cost of the novelty |
| --- | --- |
| **The mechanism** — how the work gets done | Low, if the result is familiar. The user need not understand it |
| **The workflow** — the order and shape of the work | Moderate. Requires the user to change habits, which `03-user`'s immovables may forbid |
| **The interface** — how it is operated | High and often unrecognized. Every convention broken is retraining |
| **The category** — what kind of thing this is | Highest. `05-competition`'s new-category cost, paid in `11-growth` as education |

The pattern worth aiming at: **novel mechanism, conventional interface, familiar workflow.** The difference is felt as
the problem disappearing rather than as something new to learn.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 3 (Choose), as a property of the chosen approach, and in Move 5 (Bound) where deliberate conventionality
becomes a stated non-goal.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Say which layer the novelty is in.** That single sentence predicts the adoption cost better than any assessment of
how clever the idea is.

**Inherit conventions deliberately, and record it.** Using the interface patterns the segment already knows is a
strategic decision with a real payoff: zero retraining, which is part of `03-user`'s switching cost.

**Check novelty against the immovables.** A novel workflow requiring a fixed habit, rota or system of record to change
does not get adopted. That is the most predictive question in `03-user` and it applies most sharply here.

**Cost category creation before choosing it.** Being a new kind of thing means every acquisition conversation starts
with an explanation. `11-growth` funds that, and `05-competition` required the choice to be stated.

**Distinguish novel-to-us from novel-to-the-market.** `05-competition`'s enumeration includes who tried this and
stopped. An approach that feels original and has been attempted before carries the reason it was abandoned.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Novelty is treated as value.** Users are trying to complete a job. A more original product that completes it less
well loses to a conventional one, and the originality is invisible to them.

**Interface novelty is invisible to its designer and expensive for everyone else.** The designer knows where
everything is. `04-problem`'s usability method exists to expose the gap, and it should be run before the novelty is
committed to.

**Innovation is used as the justification when the comparison did not favor an option.** `Tradeoffs.md` requires the
choice to be defended against research-derived criteria. Novelty is not one of them.

**Being first is confused with being defensible.** `05-competition` is explicit: first-mover advantage is a head start
that becomes a moat only if something accumulates during it.

**Conventionality is treated as a failure of ambition.** Choosing the familiar shape is frequently the highest-leverage
decision in the plan, because it spends the adoption budget on the one thing that is actually new.
