# MVP Solution

---

# What It Is

The narrow-tool shape considered as a **strategy in its own right**: solve one problem completely, for one segment,
and stay there.

This is distinct from `07-strategy/knowledge/MVP.md`, which draws the cut line once an approach has been chosen. Here
the narrow tool is one of the three competing options, and its distinguishing property is that it may be the final
shape rather than the first stage.

| Property | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| One problem, solved fully | Fastest to a defensible claim in `05-competition`'s positioning test |
| Small surface | Low support cost, and `13-operations` stays proportionate |
| Clear buyer conversation | Time to first customer is shortest of the five shapes |
| Limited ceiling | `06-business`'s revenue depends on segment size and price, not on breadth |
| Easily copied | Often indefensible — `Gap-Analysis.md`'s "a feature, not a company" |

The last two rows are the honest costs, and whether they matter depends entirely on the operator's goal from
`01-idea`.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 1 (Diverge) as one of the options, and again in Move 2 when scored against time to first customer and
defensibility.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Score it against the operator's goal, not against ambition.** For someone replacing a salary, a narrow tool serving
a small segment at a real price is frequently the correct answer. For a venture-scale goal it usually is not, and
`01-idea`'s `Goals.md` made that the framework's most consequential input.

**Test the end-to-end property early.** A narrow tool has to complete a job. Narrow and incomplete is the worst
position available — it inherits the small ceiling without the working solution.

**Check defensibility explicitly.** A single well-solved problem is the easiest thing for an incumbent to add. If the
answer to `05-competition`'s six-month question is "nothing", that is a stated condition, not a disqualification.

**Cost it properly.** Narrow does not mean cheap: the compliance floor, the failure paths and the support obligation
are largely fixed regardless of surface area. `13-operations` prices them the same way.

**Say whether it is a destination or a wedge.** Both are valid. A wedge needs the expansion path named — which is
`11-growth`'s and `pricing/Upgrade-Path.md`'s territory — and a destination needs the ceiling accepted.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Narrow is assumed to be a phase rather than a choice.** Treating it as stage one commits to a breadth nobody has
evidence for, and it makes the MVP a foundation rather than a test.

**A small surface is mistaken for a small business.** Price and segment size determine revenue. A narrow tool at a
high price in a professional segment can outperform a broad one sold cheaply.

**The support and compliance floor is underestimated.** A single-feature product in a regulated market still needs
audit trails, access control, incident handling and a rota. Those costs do not scale down with scope.

**It is chosen to avoid deciding.** Narrow because the problem is sharpest is a strategy; narrow because breadth is
hard to plan is deferral, and it shows up as a product with no direction attached.

**Defensibility is claimed from focus.** Serving a segment attentively is real and temporary. It is the "we would
simply be faster" row — sometimes sufficient, never durable.
