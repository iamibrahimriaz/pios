# V1

---

# What It Is

The first release intended to be **sold and supported** — as distinct from the MVP, which is intended to be
learned from.

| | Purpose | Audience | Success is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MVP** | Answer a question | A small, known group | The question answered, either way |
| **V1** | Earn revenue and hold it | The chosen segment | Customers who stay and pay |

The gap between them is not features. It is everything a product needs in order to be depended on: the failure
paths, the access controls, the support path, the data-export route, the operational readiness `13-operations`
requires.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 6 (Sequence), as the milestone after the MVP. Its scope is **gated on what the MVP taught** — writing V1's
full scope before the MVP has run is planning against an unanswered question.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Define V1 as a decision point, not a feature list.** "If the MVP shows X, V1 adds Y" is a plan. A fixed scope for
V1 assumes the MVP's result, which removes the reason for having built it.

**Include the unglamorous obligations.** Audit trails, access control, erasure paths and export — the compliance
floor from `09-technology`. These are not V2 material in a regulated product, and `06-business` established they
are never a paid tier either.

**Add the operational minimum.** A support path with a named owner, a runbook for the two most likely failures, and
a monitored alert. `13-operations` will require them; a V1 sold without them creates an obligation nobody can meet.

**Pull the second-ranked problem in only if the first is confirmed solved.** Otherwise V1 doubles the surface while
the core value remains unproven.

**Name the price it is sold at.** V1 is the first release where `06-business`' pricing is actually tested, and the
figure should be the one the model used.

---

# Where It Misleads

**V1 absorbs everything cut from the MVP.** The deferral ledger becomes a scope, and V1 grows into the product the
MVP was supposed to avoid building. Each item needs re-justifying against what was learned, not automatic promotion.

**It is planned in full before the MVP ships.** That converts the MVP into a stage of V1 rather than a test, and the
question it was asking gets quietly dropped.

**Operational readiness is deferred because it does not demo.** Support, monitoring and recovery are what make a
product sellable to a professional buyer. Omitting them produces a V1 that wins a customer and then loses them.

**Being sellable is confused with being complete.** V1 serves one segment solving one problem well. Completeness is
not a milestone; it is a direction, and `Future.md` covers why it should not be planned.

**The price is left until after V1 is built.** By then the scope has been chosen without reference to what the price
has to support, and `06-business`'s value ratio no longer holds.
