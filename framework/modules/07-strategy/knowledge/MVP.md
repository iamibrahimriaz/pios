# MVP

---

# What It Is

The smallest thing that solves the sharpest problem **end to end** for the primary persona.

Not the smallest buildable thing. Not a demo. The smallest *complete* solution to one problem for one person.

> **The end-to-end test:** can the primary persona complete the core job using only what is above the line?

If no, the cut is wrong. An MVP that cannot complete a single job teaches nothing when it ships, because nobody can
use it for its actual purpose.

Three things are recorded with the line:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The cut principle** | One sentence: what makes something MVP in this product |
| **What the MVP proves** | The specific belief it tests |
| **What it does not prove** | What remains unknown after shipping it |

---

# When It Applies

In Move 4 (Cut), after an approach has been chosen. Everything below the line goes to the roadmap — **nothing is
dropped silently.**

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Cut vertically, not horizontally.** One complete journey beats six partial capabilities. This is the same
discipline `10-execution` enforces with its demo test, and it is what makes the end-to-end test passable.

**Write the cut principle as a rule someone else could apply.** "In scope only if the primary persona cannot
complete a consultation note without it" lets a builder decide a borderline case without asking. A list of features
does not.

**State what the MVP does not prove, explicitly.** Teams routinely ship an MVP and conclude the wrong thing from
it, because nobody wrote down in advance which question it was answering. An MVP proving usability proves nothing
about willingness to pay.

**Set the MVP against the switching cost, not against zero.** `03-user` priced what leaving the status quo costs.
An MVP below that bar produces non-adoption that says nothing about the problem — the false negative
`04-problem`'s `MVP.md` warns of.

**Keep everything above the line traceable to the sharpest problem.** `08-product` will require every requirement
to trace to a ranked problem, and a line drawn loosely here produces orphans there.

---

# Where It Misleads

**"Everything is MVP" is the most common outcome and it is a refusal to decide.** It produces six months before
first contact with a user, which is the specific failure the whole module exists to prevent.

**Minimum gets optimized and viable gets dropped.** A product that half-solves the problem produces a rejection
that will be read as evidence against the problem. Viable is load-bearing.

**The line is drawn by build effort rather than by the job.** What is cheap to build and what completes a journey
are different sets, and only the second passes the end-to-end test.

**MVP becomes a synonym for version one.** It is a test with a question attached. Once it is framed as the first
release, the question disappears and the deferral ledger stops being maintained.

**The cut is presented as a research conclusion.** It commits money and months, and it is the operator's call. The
module ends at a human checkpoint for this reason.
