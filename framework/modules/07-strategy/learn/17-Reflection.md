# Reflection

---

# Overview

This module asks you to choose. These questions are about when you actually did.

---

# On the Decision

**When did you know?** Be precise. If the answer is "before module 04," the six modules
of research were producing justification rather than input — which is worth knowing, and
does not necessarily mean the choice was wrong.

**Did the analysis change anything?** A weight, a scope boundary, a sequence, the
option itself. If nothing moved, the research was decorative in this run.

**What would have changed your mind?** Answer as of before you decided. If nothing
would have, it was not a decision.

---

# On the Options

**Which option did you include to make up the number?** There is usually one. Its
presence is not a problem; presenting it as a serious candidate is.

**Which option did you not consider?** The usual omissions are: do nothing, deliver it
manually, partner with an incumbent, and build it for a much narrower group. Three of
those four win more often than teams expect.

**Was there an option that would have been better if the market were different?** That
is a timing finding worth recording — it may become the right answer in two years.

---

# On the Weights

**Where did each weight come from?** Trace them. A weight with an upstream justification
is defensible. A round number is a preference.

**Which weight would flip the result if halved?** That weight is your decision. Everyone
should know which one it is.

**Did you adjust any after seeing totals?** Honestly. This is the mechanism of
confidence laundering and it does not feel like anything at the time.

---

# On the Cut

**What hurt to cut?** If nothing did, nothing was cut.

**What did you keep because cutting it would have caused an argument?** That item is
scope that survived on politics, and it will be the one that overruns.

**Is your phase-two list a roadmap or a graveyard?** Both are legitimate. Confusing them
is not, and everyone in the room usually knows which it is except the document.

---

# On the Uncertainty

**If module 04 declared a shortfall, how does your choice reflect it?** Point at the
line. If you cannot, it did not.

**What are you betting on?** Every strategy has one central bet. Name it in a sentence.
If you cannot state it in one, either there are several — which is a risk finding — or
it has not been identified.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your comparisons never surprise you | You decide first and document after |
| Your options are always variants of building | The premise is never on the table |
| Your non-goals are always safe | You cut where it costs nothing |
| Your MVPs are small products | You optimize for shipping rather than learning |
| Your risk registers have no names | You are documenting rather than assigning |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> If you had to explain this choice to someone who preferred a different option, what
> would you point at?

If the answer is the analysis, good. If the answer is judgment or experience, that may
well be correct — some choices genuinely rest on things that cannot be written down —
but then say so plainly, rather than pointing at a comparison that was arranged to
agree.

An honestly stated judgment call is a better artifact than a laundered analysis. It can
be argued with, and it tells the reader exactly what they are trusting.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> The most useful question in this module has three words: when did you know?
>
> The answer tells you what the previous six modules were actually for.
