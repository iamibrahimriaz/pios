# Core Principles

---

# Principle Statement

> A strategy is a set of refusals.
>
> If nothing was refused, nothing was decided.

---

# Principle 1 — Generate Before You Choose

An option list assembled after the decision is decoration.

Generate genuinely different approaches first, while no answer is preferred. The test is
mechanical: would each produce a different first build?

---

# Principle 2 — No Straw Men

Two obviously weak alternatives make a count of three and defeat its purpose.

Every option must be one a competent person could reasonably choose. If a case cannot be
made for it in one honest sentence, replace it rather than count it.

---

# Principle 3 — Score Against Research, Not Preference

Every criterion in the comparison traces to a finding: the ranked problems, the gap, the
defensibility verdict, the price, the cost structure.

A score that traces to nothing is a preference with a number attached, and it will win
arguments it should lose.

---

# Principle 4 — Record What You Reject and What It Cost You

Every option had something to offer. Naming it does two things: it proves the option was
real, and it stops the rejected approach returning six months later as an unexamined
feature request.

---

# Principle 5 — The MVP Is the Smallest Complete Thing

Not the smallest buildable thing, and not a demo. The smallest thing that solves the
sharpest problem **end to end** for the primary persona.

An MVP that cannot complete one whole job teaches nothing when it ships, because nobody
can use it for its actual purpose.

---

# Principle 6 — Write What the MVP Does Not Prove

Teams ship an MVP, see weak adoption, and conclude the problem was not real — when the MVP
never tested that at all.

Recording in advance which question this release answers, and which it leaves open, is what
makes the result readable.

---

# Principle 7 — Nothing Is Dropped Silently

Everything below the line goes to the roadmap. Every excluded problem goes to non-goals.
Every rejected option goes to the record.

Silence is how scope returns: nobody remembers agreeing to exclude something, so it comes
back as an obvious omission.

---

# Principle 8 — A Risk Without an Early Warning Has Not Been Thought About

Producing the observable precursor forces the mechanism to be articulated, and the
mechanism reveals the real likelihood.

A register of risks with mitigations and no warning signs is a document. One with warning
signs is an instrument.

---

# Principle 9 — Rate Likelihood Before Impact

Rating impact first anchors the likelihood. A catastrophic risk gets quietly downgraded in
probability, because the combination is unbearable to write down.

Then check the spread. If everything is medium, nothing was assessed.

---

# Principle 10 — Do Not Launder Inherited Uncertainty

Strategy documents are written in a confident register. That register is borrowed from the
genre, not earned by the evidence.

An assumed problem stays assumed. A closeable gap stays closeable. Conditional viability
stays conditional. Carry the uncertainty forward in the words the source module used.

---

# Principle 11 — Validate Before Building When the Problem Is Unproven

If the sharpest problem was never verified, the first milestone is a test, not a build.

This is the point where the framework's honesty about evidence becomes a change in what
actually happens. Without Milestone Zero, module 04's careful labeling is a paragraph
nobody acts on.

---

# Principle 12 — Define Failure Before You Are Invested

What result would mean this should stop?

Write it now, with a number and an owner. Once building has started, the evidence arrives
into a room that has already spent too much to read it honestly.

---

# Principle 13 — The Cut Belongs to the Operator

The MVP line commits money and months. It is a commercial decision.

Presenting it as a completed research finding removes the operator's opportunity to make
it. Present options with a recommendation, then wait.

---

# Principle Hierarchy

```
Options generated
   ↓
Scored against research
   ↓
One chosen, rejections recorded
   ↓
Cut to the smallest complete thing
   ↓
Exclusions written down
   ↓
Risks and stop conditions registered
   ↓
Handed to the operator
```

Each level depends on the one above. A cut made before a choice, or a choice made before
generation, produces a document that looks like strategy and contains none.

---

# Common Violations

- Producing options to justify a decision already made.
- Including alternatives that exist only to lose.
- Scoring on preference and presenting it as analysis.
- Documenting only the chosen option.
- Calling a ranked feature list an MVP.
- Omitting what the MVP does not prove.
- Leaving non-goals unwritten.
- Rating every risk medium.
- Writing with more confidence than the research supports.
- Skipping Milestone Zero on an unproven problem.
- Presenting the checkpoint as an announcement.

---

# Self Assessment

- Did I generate before choosing?
- Could a competent person choose any of my options?
- Does every score trace to a finding?
- Did I write what the rejected options offered?
- Can the persona complete one whole job with the MVP?
- Did I say what the MVP does not prove?
- Is anything excluded silently?
- Does every risk have an early warning?
- Is my language more certain than my inputs?
- Am I asking, or telling?
