# SOM

---

# What It Is

**Serviceable obtainable market** — what could realistically be won in the first year, given one
team, one budget and one set of channels.

SOM is the only one of the three figures that is a claim about *this operator*. TAM and SAM
describe the market; SOM describes what this specific attempt can reach. That makes it the figure
`06-business` actually needs and the one most often invented.

| | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| **TAM** | The need exists |
| **SAM** | It is legal and reachable |
| **SOM** | This team, this year, through these channels |

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 3 (Size), last of the three. It is the figure `06-business` carries into revenue, and
`11-growth` later has to produce a mechanism for.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Derive it from capacity, not from a share.** Work forward from what the operator can actually do:

```
reachable contacts per month through a named route
  × plausible conversion
  × months
  = customers
  × price
  = SOM
```

That arithmetic can be argued with. "5% of SAM" cannot, because there is nothing inside it.

**Name at least one real channel.** If no route to these customers can be named here, SOM is not
small — it is unknown, and that should be recorded as such. `11-growth` will require a named
channel for every user anyway; discovering the absence now is cheaper.

**Constrain it by the operator's capacity.** A one-person team with no sales function cannot
onboard hundreds of regulated organizations in a year. Capacity is a legitimate ceiling and stating
it is not pessimism.

**Tag it, and expect it to be an assumption.** SOM is nearly always `[assumption: needs
validation]` in a pre-launch run. Marked as one, it is usable; presented as a projection, it
becomes a commitment `06-business` inherits.

---

# Where It Misleads

**SOM is the framework's single most laundered number.** It is arrived at by picking a comfortable
percentage of SAM and then reasoning backward to justify it. The tell is the absence of a
mechanism: no channel, no contact volume, no conversion assumption — just a share.

**It becomes the revenue line and then stops being questioned.** `06-business` builds on it,
`07-strategy` sets the MVP against it, and by `11-growth` its origin is forgotten. The payback
check in module 11 exists partly to catch this from the other end.

**Round percentages are a signal.** 1%, 5% and 10% appear far more often than any real capacity
calculation would produce. A number derived from contacts and conversions almost never lands on a
round share.

**Being conservative is not the same as being derived.** A small unjustified figure is no better
evidence than a large one; it is simply less exciting. Both are assumptions.
