# Market Definition

---

# What It Is

A market defined by **boundary** rather than by adjective: who, what need, where. A boundary is a
rule that can rule a borderline case in or out.

| Adjective | Boundary |
| --- | --- |
| The healthcare software market | Solo and two-doctor general practices, clinical documentation during consultations, «jurisdiction» |
| The AI productivity market | Litigation paralegals, discovery document review, England and Wales |

The first column cannot be measured, because nobody can say what is inside it. The second can be
counted, because the counting rule is written down.

Everything numeric in this module and in `06-business` inherits this boundary. A figure is only as
meaningful as the boundary it was measured within, which is why the boundary is drawn first.

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 1 (Bound), before regulation and before any sizing. It is revisited only when Frame 2
finds that regulation excludes part of what was bounded.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**State who, what need, and where.** All three. A market with no jurisdiction cannot be regulated
in Frame 2 and cannot be sized in Frame 3.

**Run the boundary test.** Take a borderline company or product and rule it in or out, with the
reason. If the boundary cannot decide the case, it has not been drawn — it has been described.

**Record what is outside, and why.** Exclusions do work later: they stop the market quietly
widening in `05-competition` when something is found that does not really compete.

**Find the category name practitioners use.** If the domain has no name for this, record that as a
finding. An unnamed market may be an unfunded one — or a genuinely new one, and the two look
identical from here.

---

# Where It Misleads

**A boundary drawn to be large is the origin of most bad sizing.** Widening the definition is the
cheapest way to make a market look attractive, and it happens by wording rather than by argument.
Every subsequent figure then measures something nobody intends to serve.

**Borrowed boundaries arrive attached to borrowed numbers.** A market report's TAM carries that
report's definition. Adopting the figure adopts the definition, usually without noticing — this is
Frame 3's borrowed-sizing failure, and it starts here.

**A boundary can be too narrow to be a market.** One customer with a need is a contract, not a
market. If the boundary contains a handful of nameable organizations, say so — the framework can
still proceed, but `06-business` and `11-growth` should be told they are pricing a relationship
rather than a market.
