# Future Outlook

---

# What It Is

A judgment on **timing**: is this early, on time, or late — and why?

It is not a forecast. The framework does not predict markets. What it does is name which timing
risk this idea carries, because early and late fail in completely different ways and are survivable
in completely different ways.

| Timing | Characteristic risk | What it demands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Early** | Buyers do not yet believe they have the problem | Education, longer sales cycles, low burn, patience |
| **On time** | Competition arrives with you | Speed, and a reason to be chosen |
| **Late** | Incumbents hold the distribution | A sharp wedge into a segment they will not defend |

All three are viable. Only an unnamed one is dangerous, because the strategy will not be shaped for
it.

---

# When It Applies

Closing Frame 4 (Trend), after the trends are collected. It is one of the inputs `07-strategy`
weighs when drawing the MVP line.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Pick one of the three and defend it in a sentence or two.** The evidence is already on the page —
this is an interpretation of the trends, not new research.

**Say what makes now different from three years ago.** If nothing does, the honest answer is "on
time at best", and possibly late. A timing claim needs a change to point at: a regulation, a cost
that fell, a behavior that shifted.

**State what would have to be true for the timing to be wrong.** This is the assumption `07-strategy`
should watch, and it belongs in the risk register as one.

**Name the consequence for scope.** Early markets need cheaper products and longer runway. Late
markets need a narrower wedge. Timing that does not change scope was not really assessed.

---

# Where It Misleads

**"We are early" is the most comfortable conclusion available**, because it explains the absence of
competitors without requiring the absence to be investigated. Frame 5's discipline applies: an empty
market with no explanation is more often an absent market than an early one.

**Being early is frequently indistinguishable from being wrong, from the inside.** The
distinguishing evidence is behavioral — people already working around the problem badly. That is
`04-problem`'s subject, and a timing claim not supported there is `[assumption: needs validation]`.

**Late is treated as disqualifying.** It is not; it is a constraint on the wedge. Plenty of viable
products enter markets with established incumbents by serving a segment those incumbents cannot
serve profitably. `Market-Gaps.md` is where that wedge is found.

**Timing gets confused with urgency.** "We must move fast" is a statement about the operator's
appetite. Timing is a statement about the market, and only one of them is a finding.
