# TAM

---

# What It Is

**Total addressable market** — everyone inside the Frame 1 boundary who has this need, ignoring
whether they can be reached, sold to, or served.

TAM answers one question: *is there a ceiling here that matters?* It is a test for a floor, not a
forecast. A TAM smaller than the operator's goal ends the idea early, which is the only decision
TAM alone can make.

| TAM is | TAM is not |
| --- | --- |
| The size of the need | The size of the opportunity |
| A ceiling check | A revenue projection |
| Bounded by Frame 1 | Bounded by ambition |

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 3 (Size), first of the three. Regulation from Frame 2 already applies to the boundary,
so a segment the law excludes is not inside TAM.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Count the units, then price them.**

```
total units with the need × realistic annual price = TAM
```

State the unit explicitly — practices, seats, claims, devices. TAM figures diverge most often
because two people counted different units and neither said which.

**Ignore reachability here, and only here.** Reachability is SAM's constraint and winnability is
SOM's. Mixing them collapses the three scopes into one number that answers nothing.

**Use one price, and name it.** The price used here should be the one `06-business` later
defends. If it changes there, this figure changes too.

**Compare it to the operator's goal from `01-idea`.** That comparison is the whole point. A TAM of
£4m is fatal to a venture-scale goal and entirely sufficient for replacing a salary.

---

# Where It Misleads

**TAM is the most inflated figure in the framework**, because widening the boundary by a few words
multiplies it and costs nothing. Every widening should be visible in `market_definition`, not
absorbed into the arithmetic.

**A large TAM is routinely treated as evidence of opportunity.** It is evidence of a need
existing at scale. Whether any of it is reachable is SAM; whether any of it is winnable is SOM;
whether anyone will pay is `04-problem` and `06-business`. TAM answers none of those.

**"1% of a huge market" is not an argument.** It is arithmetic performed backward from a desired
result, with no mechanism attached. `11-growth` requires a named channel for every user, and that
requirement is what exposes it.

**TAM in currency hides the unit count.** A big money figure built on a small population is a
pricing assumption in disguise. Always record the population next to the currency.
