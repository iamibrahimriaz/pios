# Market Gaps

---

# What It Is

A part of the bounded market that is underserved — plus, crucially, **an explanation of why it
stayed that way.**

The explanation is the finding. A gap without one is not evidence of an opportunity; it is evidence
that the search stopped early.

| Reason it persists | What it implies |
| --- | --- |
| Regulation | Real, but expensive and slow to enter |
| Economics | The segment may be unprofitable to serve at all |
| Distribution | The customers exist but cannot be reached cheaply |
| Data access | Someone else controls the necessary input |
| Incentives | Nobody in the value chain benefits from closing it |
| **No reason found** | Be suspicious of the gap, not excited by it |

Each reason has a different consequence downstream. Economics goes to `06-business`; distribution
goes to `11-growth`; data access goes to `09-technology` and `14-ai-systems`; regulation is already
in Frame 2.

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 5 (Gap), last in the module. It hands the wedge to `07-strategy` and the competitive
detail to `05-competition`.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Write the reason before the opportunity.** Reversing the order produces advocacy — the reason
gets written to be surmountable rather than to be true.

**Ask what the incumbents chose.** A gap left by a large supplier is usually a decision, not an
oversight: too small, too costly to support, too regulated, too far from their buyer. Knowing which
tells you whether they will respond when you enter.

**Keep it at market level.** Naming individual competitors and comparing their features is
`05-competition`'s work, and doing it here duplicates it without the segment and problem context
module 05 will have.

**Say who the underserved group is, in Frame 1's terms.** A gap that cannot be located in a segment
cannot be sized, reached, or built for.

**Record gaps that are real but closed to this operator.** A gap requiring a certification this
team cannot obtain is still a finding — it belongs in `scope_boundaries`, not in the opportunity
list.

---

# Where It Misleads

**The absence of competitors is read as an opportunity when it is more often a verdict.** Markets
are searched constantly. If nobody serves a visibly needy group, someone probably tried — and the
reason they stopped is the single most valuable thing this frame can find.

**Gaps found by feature comparison are usually not gaps.** "No competitor offers X" describes a
product difference. It becomes a gap only when someone is materially worse off for X's absence, and
that is `04-problem`'s evidence, not this module's.

**A gap can be closed by the time you ship.** If the reason it persists is "nobody has got round to
it", the gap has no protection at all — and `Trends.md`'s adversarial trend probably names the
supplier about to close it.

**One gap gets elevated into the whole strategy.** A gap is an entry point. Whether it supports a
business is `06-business`'s answer, and whether it justifies the MVP is `07-strategy`'s.

> **Concept Note**
>
> Explain the gap before celebrating it.

> An unexplained gap is a search that ended too soon, and it will be
> explained eventually — usually by the market, at your expense.
