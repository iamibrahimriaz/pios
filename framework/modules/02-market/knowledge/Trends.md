# Trends

---

# What It Is

A movement in the market with a **direction** and **evidence**. Without a direction it is an
observation; without evidence it is a belief.

| Observation | Trend |
| --- | --- |
| "AI is being adopted in healthcare" | "Documentation tools now ship transcription by default — three of the four main suppliers added it in the last two years [verified: product changelogs]" |
| "Practices are under pressure" | "Consultation volumes per clinician rose year on year for three consecutive years [verified: published statistics]" |

The gate requires at least three, spanning demand, supply, technology, regulation and behavior — and
at least one that **works against the idea.**

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 4 (Trend), after sizing. Trends feed `05-competition`'s expectations and `07-strategy`'s

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Write each as direction plus evidence plus consequence.** What is moving, which way, on what
basis, and what it means for this idea. The consequence column is what makes a trend usable rather
than decorative.

**Cover the five types.** Demand, supply, technology, regulatory, behavioral. Concentrating on
technology is the usual pattern, and it is the type least likely to distinguish this idea from
anyone else's.

**Find the trend that argues against.** This is a gate condition, not a courtesy. Every market has
forces pushing both ways; if none was found, the research was selective rather than complete. The
common ones: incumbents shipping the same capability, the cost of the enabling technology falling
until the feature is free, buyers consolidating suppliers, regulation tightening on exactly the
mechanism the idea depends on.

**Separate a trend from a cycle.** A movement that has reversed before is a cycle, and betting on
the current phase is a different risk. Say which one this is.

**Date the evidence.** A trend supported by four-year-old data may have completed. The date belongs
in the citation.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Trend lists are the most selectively researched part of a market analysis**, because supporting
evidence is what one goes looking for. The adversarial requirement exists specifically to
counterbalance the search itself, not the analyst's honesty.

**Broad technology trends apply equally to every competitor.** "AI is improving" is true and
strategically empty — it favors the incumbent with the distribution as much as the entrant. A trend
only matters here if it changes who wins.

**A trend can be real and irrelevant.** If it does not alter what should be built, when, or for
whom, it is context for the dossier rather than a finding. Three consequential trends beat ten
observations.

**"Everyone is talking about it" is coverage, not demand.** Attention and spend diverge routinely,
and coverage is the easier of the two to find.
