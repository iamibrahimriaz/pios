# Growth

---

# What It Is

Whether the bounded market is getting larger or smaller, and why.

Two entirely different things are called growth in product documents, and conflating them is one
of this module's characteristic errors:

| | Whose growth | Where it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Market growth** | The market's — more buyers, more spend, more need | Here, module 02 |
| **Product growth** | This product's — more users through a named channel | `11-growth` |

Market growth is context. It never supplies a mechanism for acquiring a single user. `11-growth`
names this confusion **borrowed growth**: a market's growth rate presented as if it were the
product's plan.

---

# When It Applies

In Frame 4 (Trend), as the demand-side direction. It informs `07-strategy`'s timing and
`06-business`'s outlook.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**State direction before rate.** Growing, flat, or shrinking — with evidence. A direction with a
weak source is more useful than a precise CAGR with none.

**Say what is driving it.** A market growing because of a regulatory deadline behaves nothing like
one growing because a technology got cheap: the first has a date attached, the second is
open-ended. `07-strategy` needs the cause, not the number.

**Distinguish spend growth from population growth.** More money in a market with the same number of
buyers means prices or budgets rose. That is a different opportunity — and a more fragile one — than
more buyers arriving.

**Keep growth outside the sizing arithmetic.** Size the market as it is today. If a projected
figure is needed, show it separately with the growth assumption tagged. A five-year projection folded
into TAM produces a figure describing a market that does not yet exist.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Published CAGRs are usually measuring a broader market than the one bounded in Frame 1**, and
they inherit that report's definition along with its optimism. A category-level growth rate applied
to a narrow segment is an unsupported transfer, and should be tagged `[assumption]` if used at all.

**A growing market can be a worse market to enter.** Growth attracts entrants, well-funded ones
first. `05-competition` and `Five-Forces.md` handle that; growth alone should not be read as
favorable.

**A shrinking market is not automatically fatal.** A declining market with an underserved segment
and departing incumbents can be a good place to be small. Record the direction and let
`07-strategy` weigh it.

**Growth is the easiest thing to project and the least useful thing to assume.** If the rate
matters to the decision, it needs a source; if it does not matter, it does not need a number.
