# Reflection

---

# Overview

Ask these after sizing a market you have a stake in. On someone else's market they are
merely an exercise; on your own they are diagnostic.

---

# On the Number

**Did you know roughly what the number would be before you calculated it?** Most people
do. The question is what happened when the calculation disagreed — whether you revised
the expectation or revised an input.

**Which input did you adjust last?** There is usually one you went back to. That is
the input the conclusion was resting on, and it is worth noting whether you adjusted it
because you found better information or because the total was uncomfortable.

**Would you show the chain to a skeptic?** If the answer is "with some explanation,"
identify which step needs the explanation. That step is the weak one.

---

# On the Boundary

**What did you leave inside the boundary that you know does not belong?** Almost every
market definition carries one segment that is there to keep the number respectable.

**What would the analysis look like at half the boundary?** Often better. A narrower
market produces sharper competitors, clearer segments and a more specific regulatory
picture — and the SOM barely moves, because the excluded part was never reachable.

**Did the boundary come from the idea, or did it come from the number you wanted?**
Both happen. Only one is defensible.

---

# On the Timing

**What actually changed?** Name it. If you cannot name a specific change with a rough
date, the "why now" is a feeling.

**If the timing is LATE, how did that feel?** LATE is usually received as bad news and
usually is not. A late market has educated buyers, established budget lines, and
proven willingness to pay — all of which module 06 will find useful. Early markets have
none of those and are romanticized anyway.

**What would make this EARLY?** If the honest answer is "a behavior change that has
not started," that is one of the most valuable findings the module can produce, and it
belongs in module 07's risk register rather than in a footnote.

---

# On the Regulation

**Did you look, or did you already know?** Knowing is fine. Not checking the date is
not.

**Did you hope not to find something?** This is worth being honest about. The
regulatory search is the one part of this module where the researcher has an interest
in the result, and it is the part whose omission is least visible downstream.

**Who will actually run the obligation you found?** Module 13 will ask for a name. If
no name is imaginable at this stage, that is a real finding about the shape of the
business — a solo operation cannot absorb an unlimited compliance surface, and the
place to notice that is here.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your markets are always large | You are sizing to justify rather than to plan |
| Your timing is always RIGHT | The verdict is not carrying information |
| Your trends come from the same three sources | Your view of the market has a single vantage point |
| You reach the regulatory section last and briefly | The most durable output is getting the least attention |
| Your gaps are always openings | You are treating absence as evidence |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> If this market turned out to be a quarter of the size you estimated, would the
> product still be worth building?

For a great many products the answer is yes, and discovering that removes most of the
pressure that distorts market sizing in the first place. For the rest, the answer is
no — and knowing that before module 06 is worth more than any figure this module can
produce.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> Market work is where wishful thinking is most respectable, because it comes with
> arithmetic.
>
> The reflection that matters is not about the market. It is about which number you
> wanted before you started.
