# Evaluation

---

# Overview

The test underneath these: **which numbers in your plan were measured, and where?**

---

# Exercise 1 — Apply the Closure Test

Take any growth loop diagram. Follow the arrow from the output back to the input and say
what mechanism performs each step.

**Passing looks like.** Every step is something the product does or something a user does
as part of their normal work.

**Failing looks like.** One step is "users tell their colleagues." That is a hope with an
arrow, and relabeling the diagram as a funnel is the correct outcome.

---

# Exercise 2 — Name the Place, Not the Category

For a segment you know, write three channels as specific places with specific people,
and say who is trusted in each.

**Passing looks like.** Someone from that segment would recognize all three.

**Failing looks like.** The list would fit any product in the sector. That is a category
list.

---

# Exercise 3 — Locate the Payer

For each channel, mark whether it reaches the user, the payer, or both.

**Passing looks like.** At least one reaches the payer, and you can say how.

**Failing looks like.** All of them reach users. That plan produces enthusiasm that
cannot convert, and the stall will look like a sales problem.

---

# Exercise 4 — Time It

Take a product you use and time, in minutes, from arriving at the site to having
something you would miss.

**Passing looks like.** A number, and an identified longest step.

**Failing looks like.** You stop measuring because it is not reachable in one session.
That is a finding — and it is the most common one.

---

# Exercise 5 — Compute the Implied CAC

Take a channel plan and work out what it costs per acquired customer at the stated
conversion rates. Then compare with a payback ceiling.

**Passing looks like.** A number that either clears or does not, and a stated decision
about which.

**Failing looks like.** The conversion rates came from an industry report. The arithmetic
is then correct about a different market.

---

# Exercise 6 — Find the Retention Mechanism

For any subscription product, name the specific thing that makes leaving costly.

**Passing looks like.** Something structural — accumulated history, a dependency, a
record that has to live in one place.

**Failing looks like.** "It's useful." Usefulness is why people join.

---

# Exercise 7 — Test the Gate

If the run carries a declared shortfall, point at the acquisition spending and confirm it
is conditional on the validation milestone.

**Passing looks like.** An explicit condition with a named milestone.

**Failing looks like.** Spending scheduled alongside launch. The chain from module 04 has
then ended one link short of doing anything.

---

# Rubric

| Level | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Not yet** | Lists channel categories; loops drawn without closure; value time is an adjective |
| **Working** | Channels named; conversion figures borrowed but tagged |
| **Competent** | Closure test applied; payer located; time to first value measured; implied CAC computed |
| **Fluent** | Labels a funnel a funnel, gates spend on validation, and responds to a failed payback check by revisiting price or scope |

---

# A Note on What Cannot Be Assessed Here

Whether the plan will work. Growth is the module where pre-launch assessment is weakest,
because the only real test is spending money and observing.

What is assessable is whether the plan is *falsifiable* and whether the first spend is
designed to produce information. A plan with a named load-bearing conversion assumption
and a first campaign sized to measure it is a good plan even if the assumption is wrong.
A plan without one is unimprovable regardless of outcome.

---

> **Evaluation Principle**
>
> Ask of every number: where was this measured, and on whom?
>
> In growth planning, that single question removes more error than any amount of tactical
> sophistication.
