# Reflection

---

# Overview

Growth plans are where hope enters a document with the fewest obstacles. These questions
are about where yours entered.

---

# On the Numbers

**Which figure in your plan did you measure?** For most pre-launch plans the answer is
none, and that is fine — provided the document says so.

**Which one is the plan standing on?** Usually a conversion rate. Everything else can be
wrong by half without changing the conclusion.

**Where was it measured, and on whom?** If the answer involves another company's blog
post, you know what you have.

---

# On the Loop

**Does it close?** Trace it. Slowly.

**What performs the return step?** If the answer is a person choosing to do something
promotional, that is a funnel with an arrow.

**Would you rather have a funnel that works or a loop that might?** Most successful
businesses have the first. The second is more fun to draw.

---

# On the Channels

**Where do these people actually go?** Not where you can advertise. Where they already
are, for this kind of thing.

**Who do they trust there?** Reaching people through someone they do not trust is
reaching nobody.

**Can the payer be reached at all?** If not, that is a business-model finding, not a
channel gap, and it belongs back in module 06.

---

# On Time to First Value

**Did you measure it, or estimate it?** Stopwatch, real flow, cold account.

**What is the longest step?** Almost always waiting or data entry, and almost always
fixable in the product.

**Is this a growth plan or a product fix?** For many products the highest-leverage growth
work is removing four minutes from onboarding, which this module can identify and cannot
perform.

---

# On the Spending

**What are you buying with the first campaign?** Customers, or information? Early spend
that is designed to measure the load-bearing assumption is worth several times the same
money spent to acquire.

**If module 04 declared a shortfall, is the spend gated?** Check. This is the final link
of the chain and the easiest to drop, because by now the declaration is seven modules
behind you.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your plans always contain a loop | You prefer the compounding story |
| Your conversion rates come from reports | You use available numbers rather than tagged ones |
| Your channels are categories | You list what exists |
| You have never measured time to first value | The product-side cause is invisible to you |
| Your response to weak conversion is copy and design | You work the lever you own |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> If your best channel produced twice the traffic at the same conversion, would the
> business work?

If yes, the plan is a distribution problem and the arithmetic supports spending. If no,
more traffic makes the loss larger — and the honest response is to regress to price or
scope rather than to acquire faster.

Most growth plans have never asked this, and it is answerable in five minutes with
numbers module 06 already produced.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> Growth is where a product's earlier compromises present their invoice.
>
> A high switching cost, an unclear payer, a slow first session — none of them are growth
> problems, and all of them arrive here looking like one.
