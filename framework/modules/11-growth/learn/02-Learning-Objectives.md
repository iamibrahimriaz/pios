# Learning Objectives

---

# Overview

> After this module you should be able to say where a specific group of people already
> pay attention, tell a loop from a funnel, and state what your plan implies acquisition
> costs.

---

# Knowledge Objectives

You should understand:

- What the **closure test** is and why most loops fail it
- The difference between a loop and a funnel, and why a funnel is not a lesser thing
- What **borrowed growth** is and why switching cost makes benchmarks non-transferable
- What time to first value measures, and which module actually determines it
- What a retention mechanism is, as distinct from a retention hope
- The **payback check** and what a breach obliges

---

# Thinking Objectives

The shift is from *how do we grow* to *where are these specific people, and what does
reaching them cost*.

Instead of asking:

> "Which channels should we use?"

ask:

- Where does this segment already go, and who do they trust there?
- Is the payer in this channel, or only the user?
- Does anything in normal product use produce a new user?
- How many minutes before someone has something they would miss?
- What does this plan imply per acquired customer, and does that clear the ceiling?

---

# Skill Objectives

You should be able to:

- Match a channel to a named segment with a reason, not a category
- Apply the closure test and relabel a funnel honestly
- Compute an implied CAC from a channel plan
- Measure time to first value along a real flow, in minutes
- Identify a structural retention mechanism
- Gate spending on a validation milestone when the evidence requires it

---

# Analytical Objectives

You should develop the ability to:

- Notice a conversion figure that came from a different market
- Recognize a loop diagram whose return arrow is a hope
- Tell an onboarding problem from an acquisition problem — most acquisition problems are
  the former
- Spot a retention model resting on the product simply being good

---

# Judgment Objectives

**Which channel to start with.** Not the largest. The one where the segment is reachable
by someone who can plausibly reach them, at a cost the payback ceiling permits.

**When to withhold spend.** If module 04 declared a shortfall, spending before Milestone
Zero resolves it is buying evidence you decided not to collect. The judgment is whether
the milestone genuinely resolves the uncertainty, or merely occurs before the spending.

---

# You Have Learned This When

| Signal | What it indicates |
| --- | --- |
| You name where a segment is before naming a channel | The channel question is a user question to you |
| You call a funnel a funnel | The closure test is applied honestly |
| Your conversion figures are your own or tagged | Borrowed growth is recognized |
| Time to first value is a number you measured | It is a design constraint, not a slogan |
| A failed payback check sends you to price or scope | The regress rule is internalized |

---

# What This Module Does Not Teach You

It does not teach marketing execution — campaign craft, copywriting, channel operations.
It does not define metrics; module 12 does. It does not design onboarding; module 10
did, and this module measures it.

---

> **Objectives Principle**
>
> The skill is arithmetic applied to optimism.
>
> Everything else in growth planning is tactics, and tactics are learnable anywhere. The
> discipline of checking what a plan implies per customer is not.
