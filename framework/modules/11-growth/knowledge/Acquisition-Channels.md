# Acquisition Channels

---

# What It Is

Where the segment **already is** — found, not chosen.

Every channel row cites where the segment was **observed**: `03-user`'s research, `02-market`'s trade sources,
`05-competition`'s channel analysis. A channel with no citation was chosen by convention.

> **Borrowed growth** is this module's characteristic failure: importing the playbook of a product with a different model, segment
> and price. A content-marketing-and-free-trial motion applied to something sold to hospital procurement. It is not wrong because it
> is unfashionable — it is wrong because the segment is not there and the buying process does not work that way.

And the selection rule, which is counter-intuitive:

| Compare channels on | Not on |
| --- | --- |
| Cost of one test | Projected CAC |
| Time to a usable answer | Modeled volume |
| Whether the segment is evidenced there | Whether the channel is well regarded |

Projected CAC before launch is an assumption. What is knowable is the cost of finding out.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 2 (Locate), after the growth model is chosen and the payback check has run.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Write the citation on every row.** Where this segment was seen: a forum named in `03-user`, a professional body, a conference a
competitor invests in. `05-competition/knowledge/Marketing-Comparison.md` covers the last — competitors have already paid to learn
which channels work here.

**Match the channel to the motion.** A sales-led model needs channels producing conversations; a product-led one needs channels
producing self-serve arrivals. A channel that suits neither is a channel chosen in isolation.

**State the cost and duration of one test.** That is the comparable figure, and it is knowable now.

**Name the channels deliberately not used, and those that cannot be evidenced.** The second group are open questions, not plans —
the same distinction `04-problem` draws between validated and assumed.

**Respect the sequence rule.** No channel test until activation works for the first ten, and no acquisition spend at all before
Milestone Zero completes where the problem is assumed.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Channel categories are offered as plans.** "SEO", "content marketing", "social" describe activities, not access.
`06-business/knowledge/Customer-Acquisition.md` applies the same test: if it cannot become a list of names in a week, it is not a
route.

**A competitor's channel is copied without their segment.** A channel working for a supplier selling to large trusts says nothing
about reaching single-handed practices. The citation must match the segment.

**Projected CAC drives the choice.** It is invented before launch, and choosing on it means choosing on the most confident-looking
guess.

**Every channel is pursued at once.** Each needs a test, a budget and an interpretation. Two channels run badly produce no answer
about either.

**The channel that produced the first ten is assumed to scale.** It was a personal network, and `06-business/knowledge/CAC.md` is
explicit: customer eleven is a different business.
