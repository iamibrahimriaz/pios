# Referral

---

# What It Is

Growth through existing users bringing new ones — the most commonly claimed loop and the one with the strictest precondition.

The growth model table states it directly: referral **requires users who benefit from other users joining**, and it **breaks when users
are competitors.**

| Situation | Referral viability |
| --- | --- |
| Colleagues in the same organization | Strong — they share the workflow, and joining helps both |
| A profession that shares practice openly | Moderate — reputation and generosity drive it |
| Members of a professional body or network | Moderate — the network exists, the benefit is indirect |
| **Direct commercial competitors** | None. A referral gives away an advantage |
| Isolated practitioners with no peer contact | None available — there is nobody to tell |

`03-user`'s segment research answers this, and the answer is frequently no. A single-handed practice competing for the same patients
as the practice down the road has a reason not to refer.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 5 (Loop), as a candidate loop, and only where the precondition holds.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Check the precondition against `03-user` before designing anything.** Whether these specific users benefit from peers joining is a
research question with a recorded answer.

**Distinguish intra-organization from cross-organization referral.** Inviting a colleague in the same clinic is a workflow action with
immediate mutual benefit. Recommending it to a rival practice is not, and only the first is reliable.

**Tie it to a requirement.** `Retention.md`'s discipline applies to loops too: something in `08-product` has to produce the invitation
moment, and it has to arrive at a point where the user has value to share.

**Place the invitation after activation, never before.** A user who has not received value has nothing to recommend, and asking spends
goodwill for nothing.

**Be careful with incentives in professional contexts.** Payment for referrals can breach professional or regulatory rules — a
`02-market` Frame 2 question, and not a growth decision.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Referral is assumed to be available for every product.** It is a property of the segment's social structure, and for competing
practitioners it is structurally absent regardless of product quality.

**Word of mouth is recorded as a referral loop.** It is neither a loop nor a plan — it is an outcome that may occur, with no mechanism
and no cycle time. `Growth-Loops.md`'s closure test rejects it.

**An invitation feature is treated as the loop.** The feature is the mechanism; the loop requires the invitation to be *sent*, which
depends on the precondition and on the user having value to share.

**Satisfaction is confused with willingness to recommend.** In professional settings, recommending carries reputational risk. A user can
be delighted and still not put their name to it.

**Incentives are added to compensate for an absent precondition.** Paying users to refer where referral has no natural benefit produces
low-quality referrals and, in regulated professions, may be prohibited.
