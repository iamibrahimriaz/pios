# Expansion

---

# What It Is

Growth inside customers already won, plus Move 6's first-ten list.

Three directions, each with a different precondition:

| Direction | Precondition |
| --- | --- |
| **More users in the same account** | Colleagues who share the workflow — the strongest and cheapest path |
| **More usage** | A price unit that rises with usage, from `06-business/knowledge/pricing/Pricing-Models.md` |
| **More capability** | A genuinely separable need, and a requirement that serves it — `pricing/Upgrade-Path.md` |
| A second segment | Not expansion. A `07-strategy` decision with its own channels and product work |

And Move 6's requirement, which is where this module becomes concrete:

| Per first-ten row | |
| --- | --- |
| Who | A name where possible, a specific profile otherwise |
| How reached | A specific route, not a channel category |
| Why they would say yes | Their reason, not the product's features |
| What would make them say no | From `05-competition`'s status quo analysis |

> Where the sharpest problem is assumed, these ten are the **validation sample** — Milestone Zero's population, not a sales pipeline.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 6 (Name), closing the module.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Pursue in-account expansion first.** Adding a colleague requires no new channel, no new trust and no new procurement. It is the
cheapest growth available and it is frequently a workflow dependency, which `Retention.md` counts as retention too.

**Require a mechanism before counting expansion revenue.** `06-business/knowledge/pricing/Upgrade-Path.md` is explicit: if no
requirement unlocks the upgrade, expansion is zero.

**Write the reason each of the ten would say yes in their words.** Not the feature list. `04-problem`'s cost figure and
`03-user`'s job statements are the source.

**Write the objection too, from the status quo.** `05-competition` established that whatever they do today is free, installed and
trusted. That is the objection, and it will be the same for all ten.

**Frame them as the validation sample where the problem is assumed.** Naming them as such is what keeps the first conversations honest:
they are being asked whether the problem is real, not being sold to.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Expansion is projected before any customer exists.** Net revenue retention above 100% is a figure from mature products, and borrowing
it is `Growth-Loops.md`'s borrowed growth applied to revenue.

**A second segment is called expansion.** It requires new channels, possibly new requirements and a fresh product-market question.
`07-strategy` decides it, with a trigger.

**In-account growth is assumed to be automatic.** Someone has to invite the colleague, and something has to make it worth doing.
`Referral.md`'s intra-organization case is the mechanism.

**The first ten are treated as a pipeline when the problem is unvalidated.** Then the conversations become sales calls, the answers
become polite, and Milestone Zero produces confirmation instead of evidence — which `04-problem/knowledge/validation/Interviews.md`
warns about explicitly.

**"Marketing" is offered as the plan for the first ten.** It is not a plan, and the first ten are where the product either becomes real
or does not.
