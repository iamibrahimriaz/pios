# Learning Objectives

---

# Overview

> After this module you should be able to describe what a person does today accurately
> enough that they would recognize it, state what they are trying to accomplish without
> naming a solution, and price what it would cost them to change.

---

# Knowledge Objectives

You should understand:

- The difference between a **segment** (a group that behaves differently) and a
  demographic (a group that is described differently)
- What a **job to be done** is, and why it is stated without a solution in it
- What belongs in a **current workflow**: steps, tools, people, timing, and the
  workarounds
- The five components of **switching cost**
- Why the buyer and the user can be different people with different jobs
- Why an empathy map and a persona are not the same kind of artifact, and why one of
  them is restricted in this framework

---

# Thinking Objectives

The shift is from *who are they* to *what do they do*.

Instead of asking:

> "Who is our target user?"

ask:

- What does this person do today, step by step, and with what?
- Which step do they dislike, and which one do they simply tolerate?
- What did they build for themselves — a spreadsheet, a template, a habit?
- What are they accountable for, and to whom?
- What would they have to give up to use something else?
- Who signs, and does that person share this job?

The workaround question is the highest-yield one on the list. A workaround is a user
telling you exactly where the product is, in a form they did not know they were giving.

---

# Skill Objectives

You should be able to:

- Draw segments that split on behavior, and state why one is prioritized
- Write a job with no product noun in it
- Document a workflow including the parts that happen outside any software
- Identify the tools being replaced, by name
- Estimate switching cost across all five components rather than the easy one
- Distinguish what you observed from what you were told, and label both

---

# Analytical Objectives

You should develop the ability to:

- Notice when a persona is describing the researcher's assumptions back to them
- Tell a stated preference from a revealed one
- Find the constraint behind a step that looks unnecessary
- Recognize when two "segments" are the same behavior with different job titles

---

# Judgment Objectives

**Which segment to prioritize.** The gate requires at least two segments and a stated
reason for choosing one. The reason should be about reachability, sharpness of pain, or
willingness to pay — not about size, which is the default and rarely the right one.

**How much of the workflow to document.** Enough that module 10 can design a flow and
module 05 can score the status quo. Beyond that, detail is collected because it is
available rather than because it is needed.

---

# You Have Learned This When

| Signal | What it indicates |
| --- | --- |
| Your first question is "what do you do now" rather than "what would you want" | The observational habit has formed |
| You can state a job without a noun from the product in it | The jobs discipline is real |
| You ask about renewal dates and data export | Switching cost is a number to you, not a caveat |
| Your personas contain something surprising | They came from a person rather than from a template |
| You notice when a user's workaround is better than your plan | You are reading the present instead of selling the future |

---

# What This Module Does Not Teach You

It does not teach problem prioritization — module 04 does that with this module's
outputs. It does not teach interface design. It does not tell you whether the users you
found are worth serving; that is assembled from modules 04, 05 and 06 together.

---

> **Objectives Principle**
>
> The skill is describing the present precisely enough that it constrains the future.
>
> A user section that constrains nothing has taught you nothing, however long it is.
