# Future Improvements

---

# Known Limitations

**Nothing distinguishes observed from reported.** The evidence policy tags claims by
strength, but a workflow described in an interview and a workflow watched in person
both arrive as `[verified: user]`. They are not equivalent, and module 04 treats them
identically.

**The module can complete without contact.** Every output can be produced by a
sufficiently thoughtful person who has met nobody. The gate checks that segments,
jobs, workflow and switching cost exist — not that any of them came from outside the
room. This is the module's largest structural weakness and it has no clean fix, because
a gate cannot verify that a conversation happened.

**Switching cost is a single figure.** Five components go in and one number comes out,
so the composition is lost. Module 11 needs to know *which* component dominates in
order to plan against it — an adoption problem caused by retraining is addressed
differently from one caused by contract timing.

**Sample size is invisible.** A finding from one interview and a finding from twenty
look the same in the output. No field carries `n`.

**Personas remain the most requested and least useful artifact.** They are produced
because they are expected. The framework restricts empathy maps for a structural reason
and does not apply the same scrutiny to personas, which are vulnerable to the same
failure — a template that gets filled because it has slots.

**No handling of the multi-sided case.** Products where two user groups need each other
— a marketplace, a scheduling tool, anything with a requester and a responder — get one
set of segments. The dependency between the sides is the whole product, and this module
has no structure for it.

---

# Candidate Improvements

| Candidate | What it would fix | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An `observed` / `reported` distinction on workflow claims | Module 04 could weight evidence properly | Small; one tag, and it changes what ranking means |
| Carry switching cost as five components, not one | Module 11 can plan against the dominant one | Small |
| An `n` on every user finding | Sample size stops being invisible | Small, and awkward — most honest answers will be 1 |
| A multi-sided structure | Marketplaces stop being handled by convention | Large; affects modules 04, 06, 08 and 11 |
| Subject personas to the empathy-map treatment | The template-filling failure gets named | Moderate, and contentious — personas are widely expected |
| A contact-evidence field | Makes "we met nobody" visible rather than silent | Moderate; it can be filled dishonestly, and a gameable field may be worse than none |

The last row is the honest dilemma of this module. Requiring proof of contact would
turn the framework's most important research discipline into a compliance exercise,
and compliance exercises get satisfied rather than performed.

---

# What Should Not Change

**Jobs stated without solutions.** Every request to relax this comes from the fact that
users speak in solutions. That is exactly why the discipline is here.

**The status quo profiled as a competitor.** It is the strongest competitor in most
markets and the least likely to be taken seriously.

**Switching cost as a required output.** It is the least glamorous number in the module
and the one that most often explains a failed launch. Making it optional would remove
the framework's only structural defense against assuming adoption.

**The restriction on empathy maps.** The reasoning — that a format weighting everything
equally will be filled as though everything mattered equally — applies more broadly
than this one artifact, and removing it would remove the precedent.

---

> **Improvements Principle**
>
> The deepest limitation here cannot be fixed by a gate: this module can be completed
> without meeting anyone, and the result is indistinguishable in the document.
>
> Everything else on this page is a schema change. That one is a discipline, and it
> lives with the person doing the work.
