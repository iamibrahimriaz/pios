# Reflection

---

# Overview

The questions worth asking here are all about proximity — how close you actually got,
and what you filled in from a distance.

---

# On the Evidence

**How many people did you speak to, and how many did you watch?** These are different
numbers and the second is usually zero. That is not a failure, but it should be known
rather than blurred.

**Which line in your document is doing the most work, and where did it come from?**
Trace it. In many runs the most consequential sentence about the user turns out to be
inference presented in the same voice as observation.

**What did you hear once and write as though it were general?** A single interview is
a data point and reads like a finding. The tag exists for this.

---

# On the People

**Did you like them?** Researchers unconsciously soften descriptions of people they
like and sharpen descriptions of people they find frustrating. Both distort what module
04 inherits.

**Whose behavior did you find irrational?** That is where the constraint you have not
found is hiding. Nearly always.

**Who did you not talk to because they were hard to reach?** They are usually the
segment with the most acute version of the problem, and their absence shapes the whole
run silently.

---

# On the Jobs

**Which job did you write from what someone said, and which from what someone did?**
Mark them differently, at least in your own copy.

**Which job survived being stripped of its solution?** If none did, the interviews
collected requests rather than jobs — a normal outcome that is worth naming rather than
absorbing.

---

# On the Switching Cost

**What would it take for you to change the tool you complain about most?** Answer
honestly, in hours. Then compare with the number you wrote for your users. If theirs is
lower than yours, ask why you assumed they move more easily than you do.

**Which component did you not price?** There is usually one. Perceived risk is the most
frequently skipped, because it has no invoice.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your users always resemble you | You are researching the segment you can imagine |
| Your workflows start at a screen | You are documenting the part you can change |
| Your jobs sound like feature names | You are accepting first framings |
| Your switching costs are low | You are optimistic about adoption, which shows up as a growth failure later |
| Your personas never surprise anyone | You are producing agreement rather than information |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> Would the person you described recognize themselves, including the parts that are
> not flattering?

Real user research contains something the user would rather you had not noticed — a
workaround they know is unwise, a step they skip, a tool they were told not to use.
A document with none of that is a document about an audience rather than about people.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> The distance between you and the user is the largest uncontrolled variable in this
> module.
>
> Nothing in the framework measures it. Only you can, and only by asking how much of
> what you wrote you actually saw.
