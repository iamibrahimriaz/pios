# Usage Frequency

---

# What It Is

How often the job actually arises — recorded per job, in the natural unit of the work.

Frequency is one of the highest-leverage facts this module produces, because three later modules
depend on it and none of them can derive it:

| Module | What frequency decides |
| --- | --- |
| `06-business` | Whether a subscription is defensible, or a per-use price is honest |
| `11-growth` | Whether habit is available as a retention mechanism at all |
| `12-metrics` | The window for every rate — daily, weekly and per-episode are different products |

| Frequency | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Many times a day** | Habit forms; speed dominates; small friction is fatal |
| **Weekly** | Habit is weak; reminders and triggers matter |
| **Monthly or per-episode** | Habit is unavailable; the product must be findable and re-learnable each time |
| **Once** | Retention is not a lever; the value must land immediately |

---

# When It Applies

In Move 5 (Job), as a required attribute of each job, drawn from what Move 4 observed.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Count the job, not the product.** How often the underlying situation occurs is a fact about the
person's work and is stable. How often they would open the product is a prediction, and this module
does not make predictions.

**Use the natural unit.** Per consultation, per claim, per shift, per month-end. Converting everything
to "daily active" imposes a measure that may not correspond to anything real, and `12-metrics` inherits
the distortion.

**Record the distribution, not just the average.** Work that clusters — month-end, Monday morning, the
last hour of a clinic — sets the load figure `09-technology` sizes against. An average hides peaks
entirely.

**Say what triggers it.** A job with an external trigger needs no habit; the trigger does the work.
That is the strongest retention position available to a low-frequency product, and it is only visible if
the trigger is recorded.

**Mark low frequency plainly.** It is not a defect. It is a constraint that rules out one class of
retention mechanism and rules in another, and hiding it produces a `11-growth` loop that cannot close.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Frequency gets inflated by imagining engagement.** A monthly job does not become a daily product
because a dashboard was added. Adding an unrequested reason to return is `11-growth`'s capability
theater in an earlier form.

**Reported frequency drifts toward the round and the ideal.** "Twice a day" often means "when I
remember". Tag reported figures as reported; `Behaviors.md` ranks why.

**Low frequency is treated as disqualifying.** Tax software, annual audits and incident tools are all
low frequency and viable. What low frequency actually rules out is habit as a retention mechanism —
`11-growth` then needs a trigger, a switching cost, or an integration instead.

**The buyer's frequency is not the user's.** A tool used daily by staff and reviewed annually by the
purchaser has two frequencies, and `06-business` needs the buyer's one to understand renewal.
