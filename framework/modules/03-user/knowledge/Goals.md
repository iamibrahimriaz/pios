# Goals

---

# What It Is

What the person is trying to achieve — separated into three kinds, because they diverge and the
divergence explains most adoption failures.

| Kind | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Stated goal** | "Spend less time on paperwork" |
| **Measured goal** | Patients seen per session — what their performance is judged on |
| **Personal goal** | Leave on time; not be the one who made a mistake |

Where the measured goal and the stated goal disagree, **the measured goal wins.** A product that
improves something nobody is assessed on is adopted out of goodwill, and goodwill does not survive a
busy week.

This is distinct from `01-idea/knowledge/Goals.md`, which covers the operator's and the product's
goals. This file is only about the user's.

---

# When It Applies

In Move 3 (Embody), as rows of the persona, and again in Move 5 (Job) — a job whose outcome clause
conflicts with the measured goal will not be pursued however well it is served.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Find what they are measured on, and by whom.** In institutional and regulated markets this is the
highest-value row in the persona and the one most often missing. It may be a target, a rota, an audit,
or a manager's opinion.

**Record the personal goal separately.** Not being blamed, not looking incompetent, and getting home
are real motivators that no formal objective lists. They frequently determine whether a tool is trusted
enough to be relied on.

**Check each job against the measured goal.** If a job helps the person but not their measure, mark
that tension — `11-growth` will need it to explain why an obviously useful product is not spreading.

**Distinguish the user's goal from the buyer's.** They are different people in most institutional
sales, and the buyer's goal is usually cost or risk while the user's is time or effort. Both go in,
labeled.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Stated goals are collected because they are volunteered.** They are the polite, plausible version.
The measured goal is what actually allocates the person's attention, and it takes a specific question
to surface.

**Goals get inferred from the role title.** What a job is nominally for and what its holder is
assessed on diverge routinely — sometimes in direct opposition. Inference here should be tagged as
inference.

**A goal can be served while the person is made worse off.** Reducing documentation time by producing
output they then have to check carefully has moved the work, not removed it. `12-metrics`'
counter-metrics exist for this, and this row is where the risk becomes visible.

**Aggregating goals across a segment flattens the conflict.** Where the user's and the buyer's goals
oppose each other, a single merged goal list hides the most consequential fact in the module.
