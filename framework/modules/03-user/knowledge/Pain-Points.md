# Pain Points

---

# What It Is

Specific places where the current way of working costs the person something — time, money, risk,
standing, or attention.

The framework cares about one distinction above all others: **does the pain have a cost the person
already pays?** If it does, it is a candidate problem. If it does not, it is an irritation, and
irritations do not fund products.

| Irritation | Pain |
| --- | --- |
| "The interface is dated" | Forty minutes of unpaid work after every clinic |
| "It could be quicker" | One in ten claims rejected, each taking an hour to resubmit |
| "I wish it were nicer" | A near-miss on a prescription, once, and the fear of the next one |

---

# When It Applies

Collected during Move 4 (Observe) as the friction column of the journey. `04-problem` then takes them
over: ranking, quantifying and verifying are its work, not this module's.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Locate every pain at a step.** A pain that cannot be placed in the journey is a sentiment. Placing
it also makes it addressable — `08-product` can only build against something with a location.

**Record what it costs, in the unit the person feels.** Minutes, pounds, rejected claims, evenings.
An unquantified pain cannot be ranked, and `07-strategy`'s MVP line is drawn on the ranking.

**Record who bears it.** Where the person suffering is not the person buying, that gap governs
`06-business` and `11-growth`. It is visible here and expensive to discover later.

**Note frequency alongside severity.** A daily small pain usually outranks an annual large one for a
product decision, and the two get conflated whenever only severity is recorded.

**Keep the ranking for module 04.** This module supplies evidence; module 04 ranks it with the
quantification and verification it requires. Ranking here without that discipline produces a priority
order that later modules trust more than it deserves.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Pain gets collected by asking, and asking produces the weakest kind of evidence.** People report the
pain that is easiest to articulate, not the largest. The costly one is often normalized to the point of
invisibility — "that's just how it is" is where the real pain hides.

**Interface complaints crowd out workflow costs.** Aesthetic and usability irritations are the most
readily volunteered and the least likely to drive a purchase. A product built on them competes on
polish against incumbents who own the distribution.

**Severity is inferred from how it was expressed.** Someone calm about a serious cost and someone
animated about a trivial one are both common. Weight by the cost, not by the volume.

**A pain nobody is willing to pay to remove is still a real pain.** That is a `06-business` finding,
not a reason to omit it — but it must not be counted as validation. `04-problem`'s verification bar
exists for exactly this.
