# Mission

---

# What It Is

What the product does now, for whom, and to what end. One sentence, present tense.

The difference from vision is tense and scope:

| | Tense | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vision** | Future | The changed world |
| **Mission** | Present | What this product does today |

> **Mission:** We help single-handed GPs complete consultation notes during the appointment.
>
> **Vision:** Nobody writes up notes after hours.

A mission that cannot be said in the present tense is a vision wearing the wrong label — and
that matters, because a mission is supposed to be checkable against what actually exists.

---

# When It Applies

In Pass 6 (Bound), and revisited whenever `07-strategy` changes the MVP scope. A mission
describing capabilities that were cut is a mission that has stopped being true.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Name the person, the action and the end.** Who, what it does for them, and what that achieves.
All three, or it is a slogan.

**Keep it true of the current scope.** If the MVP does one thing, the mission describes that one
thing. A mission broader than the product is an implicit promise, and users read it as one.

**Use it as a scope test.** When a capability is proposed, ask whether the mission would need
rewriting to accommodate it. If it would, that is a strategy decision — module 07's — rather than
an addition.

**Write it after the boundaries, not before.** A mission written first becomes something the
scope is stretched to match.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Mission and vision get merged, and the merged version is always the vision.** The product then
has no present-tense description of itself, which is the sentence a builder, a user and a buyer
each need most.

**A mission can be quietly widened to cover new work.** Each widening is small; the sum is a
product with no boundaries. Rewriting the mission is a decision worth noticing, not a formality.

**Missions are frequently written for the company rather than the product.** "We are building the
future of clinical software" describes an organization's ambition and tells nobody what the
product does.
