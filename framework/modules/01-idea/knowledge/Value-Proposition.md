# Value Proposition

---

# What It Is

A claim about what changes for a specific person, stated so that it could be wrong.

The form that survives later scrutiny:

> For **«who»**, this **«does what»** instead of **«current way»**, which is worth
> **«what»** to them.

The third clause is what makes it a proposition rather than a description. Without a named
alternative there is nothing to be better than, and `05-competition` will have to invent one.

---

# When It Applies

In Pass 2 (Separate) and Pass 6 (Bound). It is written from the problem statement, not from the
feature list.

`06-business` turns it into a price. `05-competition` tests it against the status quo. Both need
the "instead of" clause.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Name the alternative honestly, and include doing nothing.** The realistic alternative for most
professional software is not a competitor — it is the spreadsheet, the paper form, the assistant,
or continuing to accept the cost.

**State the value in the user's units.** Time, risk, money, or effort — whichever the person
actually counts. A clinician may count minutes; their practice may count claim rejections. Those
are different propositions to different stakeholders.

**Separate value to the user from value to the buyer.** Where the roles differ, write both. A
product bought for compliance and used for convenience needs both claims, and `06-business`
prices against the buyer's.

**Keep it a claim, not a promise.** "Saves forty minutes a clinic" is a testable claim tagged as
an assumption. "Transforms clinical documentation" is a slogan and cannot be tested at all.

---

# Where It Misleads

**A value proposition written before research describes a hope.** That is acceptable here, and
becomes dishonest only if the register hardens as it travels. `07-strategy`'s
confidence-laundering check exists because this sentence is the one most likely to be restated
with growing certainty.

**Value is not the same as improvement.** A product can be measurably better and still not worth
switching to, because switching has a cost the proposition ignores. `05-competition` measures
that cost; this module should at least acknowledge it exists.

**The stated value is often the builder's value, not the user's.** "Structured data" is valuable
to a system. It is not, by itself, valuable to the person typing it.
