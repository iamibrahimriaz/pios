# Goals

---

# What It Is

What success would consist of — stated separately for two different parties, because they are
routinely confused.

| | Whose goal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operator goal** | The person building it | A business supporting two people within eighteen months |
| **Product goal** | The product itself | Consultation notes completed before the patient leaves |
| **User outcome** | The person using it | Leaving the clinic on time |

The operator's goal shapes the strategy — a lifestyle business and a venture-scale attempt make
different products from the same idea. The product's goals become metrics in module 12. The user
outcome is what the product is *for*.

---

# When It Applies

In Pass 3 (Locate) and at the checkpoint. The operator's goal is an **operator question** and
cannot be inferred.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Ask the operator's goal explicitly.** It changes almost everything downstream:

| Operator goal | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Replace a salary | A narrow product, a small segment, and low burn — modules 06 and 11 are shaped around margin, not growth |
| Build something venture-scale | A large market becomes a requirement, and module 02's sizing becomes decisive |
| Sell the tool to one known buyer | Market sizing barely matters; the relationship does |
| Learn, or scratch an itch | Cost ceilings matter more than growth loops |

A run that does not ask this will default to growth-oriented answers, and those may be wrong
for the operator's actual intent.

**Keep product goals observable.** "Delight clinicians" is not a goal. "Notes completed before
the patient leaves, in over half of consultations" is one, and module 12 can measure it.

**Write no more than three product goals.** More than three means none of them decides anything.

**Separate the goal from the mechanism.** "Use AI to summarize notes" is a mechanism. The goal is
whatever the summarizing is for, and stating it that way leaves module 14 free to find that a
form field works better.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Operator goals go unstated because they feel private.** Ambition, timescale and appetite for
risk all shape the strategy, and a framework that does not ask will substitute its own
assumptions — usually toward growth.

**Product goals drift toward what is measurable rather than what matters.** Module 12 guards
against this with the vanity check; the wording chosen here is where the drift starts.

**A goal can be achieved while the product fails.** "Reduce documentation time" is achievable by
a product nobody trusts enough to use. Module 12's counter-metrics exist for that reason.
