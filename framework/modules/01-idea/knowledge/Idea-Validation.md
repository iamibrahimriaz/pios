# Idea Validation

---

# What It Is

Establishing whether the beliefs an idea rests on are true — cheaply, and before they are
expensive.

Validation is not approval. It resolves specific assumptions, one at a time, and its output is
usually a partial answer with a stated confidence rather than a verdict.

**What can and cannot be validated at this stage**

| Question | Validatable early | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does this difficulty exist? | Yes | Ask people who would have it |
| Does it cost what we think? | Yes | Ask them to quantify it |
| Is it their biggest cost? | Yes | Ask what they would fix first |
| Would they pay? | Partly | Stated willingness is weak evidence |
| Would they switch? | Rarely | Switching behavior is poorly predicted by intention |
| Will they retain? | No | Requires a product and time |

---

# When It Applies

This module records the method per assumption; it does not execute it. `04-problem` carries out
what it can, declares a shortfall where it cannot, and `07-strategy` sequences the rest as
Milestone Zero.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Choose by cost of finding out, not by rigor.** Ten conversations beat a two-hundred-person
survey for a problem assumption, and both beat a prototype. The cheapest test that could change
the decision is the right one.

**Match the method to the claim:**

| Assumption about | Method |
| --- | --- |
| The problem existing | Conversations with people who would have it |
| The workflow | Observation, not description — people misreport their own process |
| The priority | Asking what they would fix first, unprompted |
| Willingness to pay | A price conversation, understanding that it is weak evidence |
| Feasibility | A technical spike, not a discussion |

**Write what result would refute it.** A validation method with no failing condition produces
confirmation, because that is what open-ended enquiry into your own idea tends to produce.

**Ask about the last time, not about the general case.** "How do you usually handle notes?"
produces a tidy account of an untidy process. "Walk me through what happened after your last
clinic" produces what actually happened.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Validation is where confirmation bias does its best work.** An idea's owner asking about their
own idea will find support. Neutral questions, unprompted priorities, and a written refutation
condition are the only real defenses.

**Stated intention is not behavior.** "Yes, I would use that" is close to free to say. It is
evidence that the problem is recognizable, not that the product will be adopted — and the
framework treats it that way deliberately.

**Ten conversations is a small sample and a large improvement.** It will not establish market
size. It reliably distinguishes "nobody has this problem" from "everybody has this problem", and
that is the distinction that matters most at this stage.

**Some things genuinely cannot be validated before building.** The honest response is a declared
shortfall, a lowered confidence, and validation sequenced first — not a stronger claim.
