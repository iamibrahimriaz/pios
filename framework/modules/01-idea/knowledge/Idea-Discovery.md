# Idea Discovery

---

# What It Is

Where the idea came from — and therefore how much is already known about it.

Origin is evidence. An idea from the operator's own daily work arrives with real observation
behind it; an idea from noticing a technology arrives with none. Both are legitimate starting
points, and they need different amounts of research.

| Origin | What comes with it | What is usually missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lived experience** | Real observation of one context | Whether others share it |
| **Observed for others** | A described workflow | What is actually done, versus described |
| **Complaint pattern** | Repetition across people | Whether it is the sharpest problem |
| **Technology first** | A capability | Any problem at all |
| **Market gap** | A competitor's absence | Why the gap exists |
| **Analogy** | A model that worked elsewhere | Whether this domain is similar |

---

# When It Applies

In Pass 1 (Capture). Recording the origin takes one line and calibrates every module after it.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**Ask where the idea came from, and write the answer down.** It sets the starting confidence.
An operator who has done the job for a decade is a strong proxy source for `03-user` — the
strongest available short of primary research — and that should be recorded as such rather than
discovered later.

**Treat technology-first origins with particular care.** "Models can now transcribe consultations"
is a capability, not a problem. The idea has to acquire a problem, and Pass 2's separation will
often reveal there is not one yet — which is a finding worth having in the first hour rather than
the sixth week.

**Treat market-gap origins as questions.** A gap that nobody has filled sometimes exists because
it cannot be filled profitably, or because regulation forbids it, or because incumbents tried and
withdrew. `05-competition` asks why the gap exists, and the answer is occasionally the whole
story.

**Treat analogy origins as one hypothesis, not two.** "Like «product» but for «industry»" carries
an assumption that the industries behave alike. Name it and register it.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Lived experience generalizes badly.** One person's workflow is one workflow. The operator's
experience is excellent evidence about the operator's setting and unreliable about others' — and
the risk grows with how confident they are.

**Repetition of a complaint is not proof of priority.** People complain most about what is most
visible, not what costs most. `04-problem` scores frequency against severity for that reason.

**An idea's origin is often reconstructed.** Founders reinterpret where an idea came from once
they are committed to it. Ask early, before the story settles.
