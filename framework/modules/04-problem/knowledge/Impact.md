# Impact

---

# What It Is

How far the problem reaches — how many people in the segment have it, and who else bears a consequence.

Score and cost describe one person's experience. Impact describes the spread, and the two answer different
questions:

| | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Score** | How badly does this hurt the person who has it? |
| **Impact** | How much of the segment has it, and who else is affected? |

| Reach | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Universal in the segment** | A product requirement — everyone needs it, so it is table stakes rather than a differentiator |
| **A large minority** | The strongest position: worth building, and not already served |
| **A small minority** | Possibly a real problem in a segment you have not yet identified |
| **One organization** | A consulting engagement, honestly described |

---

# When It Applies

Alongside Stage 3 (Score) and again in Stage 5 (Sharpen) — the sharpest problem should be sharp *and* widely
borne, and where those diverge the choice needs defending.

---

# How to Apply It Here

**State the proportion of the segment, and its basis.** "Most solo practices" tagged `[inferred: three of
four interviewed]` is honest and usable. An untagged "most" is an assumption in a quantifier.

**Name the parties beyond the user.** Colleagues who wait on the output, the patient or customer at the end,
the employer carrying the risk, the regulator who audits it. Each one is a potential buyer or a potential
objector, and both matter to `06-business`.

**Check impact against `02-market`'s sizing.** A problem affecting a tenth of the segment means SAM was sized
for a market ten times the addressable one. That reconciliation belongs here, where it is cheap.

**Distinguish breadth from breadth of agreement.** A problem everyone has and nobody prioritizes is different
from one a subset has and acts on urgently. `Current-Solutions.md` distinguishes them.

**Say when a problem indicates a different segment.** A problem borne intensely by a small minority is often a
correctly identified problem attached to the wrong segment — which is a `03-user` finding, not a reason to
discard it.

---

# Where It Misleads

**Universal problems are the most attractive to describe and the least defensible to build on.** If everyone
has it, incumbents know, and something already addresses it — adequately enough that nobody replaced them.
`Current-Solutions.md` will usually show a working workaround.

**Impact is inferred from the size of the market rather than measured.** "All 8,000 practices face this" is a
population count wearing an evidence tag it has not earned. The population is verified; the incidence is not.

**Third-party impact gets omitted because those parties were not interviewed.** In regulated and institutional
markets the affected third party is frequently the one with the budget, and their absence from the analysis is
why value cases fail to land.

**Breadth is treated as a substitute for depth.** A widely borne mild problem loses to a narrowly borne severe
one nearly every time, because only the second provokes action. The score, not the reach, predicts behavior.
