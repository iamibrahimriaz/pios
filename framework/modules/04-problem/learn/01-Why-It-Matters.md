# Why It Matters

---

# Overview

Products do not usually fail because they were built badly. They fail because they
solved something that was not a problem, or was a problem for too few people, or was a
problem that people had already solved well enough.

This module is where that gets tested, and it is the last cheap place to find out.

---

# Why This Is the Checkpoint

Look at what has happened so far. Module 01 stated a premise. Module 02 drew a boundary
and found some constraints. Module 03 described who is inside it and what they do.

All of that is **description**. None of it establishes that anyone has a problem worth
paying to remove.

Module 05 will map competitors, module 06 will build a business model, module 07 will
choose a solution — and each of those consumes the ranked problems from here as though
they were established. If the ranking rests on inference, everything after it is a
well-constructed argument about something nobody needs.

> This is the only module whose job is to say no.

---

# The Three Axes, and Why Frequency Usually Wins

A problem is scored on frequency, severity, and what people do about it today.

Teams instinctively rank by severity, because severe problems are dramatic and easy to
describe. Frequency generally matters more:

| | Severe, once a year | Mild, three times a day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total pain | Concentrated | Larger in aggregate |
| Memory of it | Vivid | Constant |
| Existing workaround | Rarely | Almost always |
| Willingness to change process | Low — it is exceptional | High — it is the process |

The last row is the decisive one. A once-a-year problem does not justify changing how
you work. A three-times-a-day problem is how you work, and improving it is worth
disruption.

---

# Why the Workaround Is the Strongest Pre-Launch Signal

Someone built a spreadsheet. Someone made a template. Someone has a naming convention
they enforce on colleagues. Someone checks a thing manually every morning.

Each of these is a person who has already paid for a solution, in the only currency
available before your product exists. That is worth more than any number of people
agreeing in an interview that the problem sounds annoying.

The corollary is uncomfortable and worth holding: **a problem with no workaround may
not be a problem.** If it were painful and frequent, someone would have done something.

---

# Why Inference Cannot Be Promoted

The framework's central prohibition — an assumption must never be smoothed into a fact
— has its sharpest application here.

```
"Clinicians dislike documentation"          → reasonable, and inferred
"Three of four clinicians described the
 end-of-day write-up as the worst part of
 their day, unprompted"                     → verified, and n=4
```

The first sentence is probably true. It cannot be ranked, because it has no frequency,
no severity anyone stated, and no workaround attached. Treating it as verified produces
a ranking with the same structure as a real one and none of the content — and nothing
downstream can tell the difference.

---

# Why `declared_shortfall` Exists

Sometimes there is no verified problem. The operator has no access to users yet, the
population is hard to reach, or the research simply has not happened.

The framework has two bad options and one workable one:

| Option | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Halt the run | Often disproportionate. A great deal of useful work can still be done |
| Pass quietly with inference | Fatal. Every later module inherits invented evidence |
| **Declare the shortfall** | The gap becomes a visible, traveling finding |

A declared shortfall states plainly: the verified list is empty or short, here is why,
here is what would fill it, and here is the validation plan. The run continues, and
module 07 is then required to weight it — typically by favoring approaches that survive
the problem being wrong.

That is what makes the mechanism honest rather than a loophole. **The declaration has
to change something downstream.** A shortfall declared and then ignored is worse than
one never declared, because it produces documentation of rigor without the rigor.

---

# What Skipping This Stage Costs

| Skipped | Surfaces as |
| --- | --- |
| Ranking | Module 07 chooses against nothing; the choice becomes preference |
| The verified/inferred distinction | Every downstream conclusion inherits invented certainty |
| Workaround analysis | The strongest available evidence is left on the table |
| The validation plan | Nobody knows what would resolve the uncertainty, so nobody does it |

---

# What This Module Does Not Do

It does not propose solutions — that is module 07, three modules later, deliberately.
It does not decide whether the business works. It does not validate anything itself;
it produces the plan that would.

---

> **Why It Matters Principle**
>
> Everything before this module is description. Everything after it is commitment.
>
> This is the joint between them, and the only honest way through it is to be exact
> about what is actually known.
