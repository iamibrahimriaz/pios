# Reflection

---

# Overview

This module asks you to say what you do not know. These questions are about what
happened when you tried.

---

# On the Evidence

**Which claim did you most want to mark as verified?** There is usually one. Whatever
it is, it is the premise of the product, and the pressure you felt is the pressure the
framework exists to resist.

**What is the actual n?** Write it. For most pre-launch runs it is between two and six,
and a document that does not say so implies a larger number by omission.

**Which piece of evidence are you leaning on hardest?** If it came from a single
conversation, note that it is currently carrying the whole run.

---

# On the Ranking

**Did the ranking surprise you?** If not, ask whether you scored or whether you sorted.

**Which problem did you want to win?** Naming it is useful, even after the fact. It
tells you which scores to re-examine.

**Which problem did you drop, and why?** Sometimes the reason is evidence and sometimes
it is that the problem was unappealing to solve. Only one of those is a finding.

---

# On the Shortfall

**Did you consider declaring one?** If you passed the gate comfortably, ask whether the
comfort was earned. Three verified problems is a demanding criterion at this stage of a
real project.

**If you declared one, what did module 07 do with it?** Go and look. A shortfall that
did not change the strategy comparison was not carried — it was filed.

**How did declaring it feel?** For most people, like admitting failure. It is worth
noticing that reaction, because it is the reaction that produces the seventh mistake on
the previous page.

---

# On the Validation Plan

**Would you actually run it?** A plan written to satisfy the gate reads identically to
one written to be executed. You are the only person who knows which you produced.

**What would you do if it disconfirmed the top-ranked problem?** Answering now, before
any result exists, is worth more than answering later — because after the result, the
answer will be negotiated.

---

# On Your Own Pattern

| Look for | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Your verified lists are always full | You are promoting inference under pressure |
| Your top problem is always the interesting one | The ranking is confirming rather than deciding |
| Your validation plans are always interviews | You are choosing the method that cannot disconfirm |
| You have never declared a shortfall | Either exceptional research access, or an unexamined reluctance |
| Your evidence n is never stated | The document is implying more than you know |

---

# The Question Worth Returning To

> If the top-ranked problem turned out not to exist, how much of the rest of this run
> would still stand?

This question has a specific practical use. Its answer is exactly what module 07 needs
in order to weight the solution comparison, and a run that can answer it — even
informally — makes a materially better strategic choice than one that cannot.

In the worked example threaded through this framework's resources, the answer to that
question is what caused a service-first approach to beat an AI product. Not a
preference. An honest reckoning with an empty verified list.

---

> **Reflection Principle**
>
> The discomfort of writing down what you do not know is the entire cost of this
> module.
>
> Everything it saves is downstream, invisible, and much larger.
