# Best Practices

## Overview

Best practices define **how Product Intelligence OS expects research to be performed**.

Unlike principles, which rarely change, best practices evolve as the framework matures.

Every completed project should improve these practices.

Following these guidelines helps produce research that is:

- Consistent
- Explainable
- Evidence-based
- Reusable
- Implementation-ready

---

# Practice 1 — Understand Before Acting

Never begin research immediately.

First understand:

- The product idea
- The business objective
- The target users
- The expected outcome
- Existing constraints

A misunderstood problem cannot produce a good solution.

---

# Practice 2 — Clarify Requirements Early

Whenever information is incomplete:

- Ask concise, high-value questions.
- Explain why each question matters.
- Avoid assumptions whenever clarification is possible.

Good research begins with clear requirements.

---

# Practice 3 — Research Before Recommending

Recommendations should never come first.

Follow this order:

```
Understand

↓

Research

↓

Analyze

↓

Recommend
```

Never reverse this sequence.

---

# Practice 4 — Focus on the Problem

Research should always begin with the problem, not the solution.

Identify:

- Who experiences the problem.
- Why it exists.
- How often it occurs.
- How it is currently solved.
- Why current solutions are insufficient.

A product exists to solve problems, not to showcase features.

---

# Practice 5 — Think Like Multiple Experts

While researching, think from different perspectives:

- Product Manager
- Customer
- Business Owner
- UX Designer
- Software Architect
- Security Engineer
- Operations Team
- Support Team

Combining perspectives produces better decisions.

---

# Practice 6 — Compare Before Choosing

Never recommend the first acceptable solution.

Whenever possible:

- Generate multiple alternatives.
- Compare strengths.
- Compare weaknesses.
- Evaluate trade-offs.
- Justify the final recommendation.

The best solution emerges through comparison.

---

# Practice 7 — Distinguish Facts from Assumptions

Every important statement should be clearly identified as one of the following:

- Fact
- Evidence
- Assumption
- Hypothesis
- Recommendation

Transparency increases trust.

---

# Practice 8 — Explain the Reasoning

Every important recommendation should answer:

- Why this solution?
- Why not another?
- What evidence supports it?
- What risks remain?
- What assumptions exist?

Recommendations without reasoning provide limited value.

---

# Practice 9 — Prefer Simplicity

Choose the simplest solution that fully solves the problem.

Avoid:

- Over-engineering
- Unnecessary features
- Excessive complexity
- Premature optimization

Complexity should always have measurable value.

---

# Practice 10 — Design for Reuse

Whenever new knowledge is created:

- Generalize the learning.
- Improve templates.
- Improve playbooks.
- Improve examples.
- Improve checklists.

Avoid creating one-time documentation.

---

# Practice 11 — Think Long Term

Every recommendation should consider:

- Future maintenance
- Product evolution
- Team growth
- Technical debt
- Scalability
- Business sustainability

Optimize for long-term success.

---

# Practice 12 — Make Research Traceable

Every important conclusion should be traceable back to:

- Evidence
- Analysis
- Assumptions
- Decision process

Future reviewers should understand how the conclusion was reached.

---

# Practice 13 — Review Before Delivery

Before delivering research:

- Verify consistency.
- Review assumptions.
- Check for missing information.
- Improve unclear sections.
- Confirm recommendations align with objectives.

Self-review is mandatory.

---

# Practice 14 — Learn From Every Project

Every completed project should contribute back to Product Intelligence OS.

Capture:

- Lessons learned
- Better questions
- Better methodologies
- Better templates
- Better examples
- Better workflows

Knowledge should continuously improve.

---

# Practices to Avoid

Avoid the following behaviors:

- Starting with solutions.
- Assuming requirements.
- Skipping research.
- Blindly copying competitors.
- Ignoring business goals.
- Ignoring user needs.
- Recommending unnecessary complexity.
- Treating assumptions as facts.
- Delivering work without review.

---

# Daily Working Habits

During every research session:

- Stay objective.
- Be curious.
- Challenge assumptions.
- Verify important information.
- Keep documentation organized.
- Record important decisions.
- Think about long-term maintainability.

Small habits produce consistently better research.

---

# Expected Outcomes

Following these best practices should result in:

- Better product research
- Better product decisions
- Better documentation
- Higher consistency
- Greater stakeholder trust
- Continuous framework improvement

---

# Self Assessment

Before completing any research task, ask:

- Did I fully understand the problem?
- Did I gather enough information?
- Did I compare alternatives?
- Did I separate facts from assumptions?
- Did I explain my reasoning?
- Did I review my work?
- Did I identify opportunities to improve the framework?

If any answer is **No**, continue refining the research.
