# Research Template

---

# Overview

Every research document within Product Intelligence OS should follow a consistent structure.

A standardized template ensures that:

- Research is easier to understand.
- Important sections are never forgotten.
- Different projects remain consistent.
- AI agents produce predictable outputs.
- Future contributors can quickly navigate documentation.

The template may be extended for specific domains, but the core structure should remain consistent.

---

# Standard Document Structure

Every research document should contain the following sections.

```
Metadata

↓

Executive Summary

↓

Objective

↓

Scope

↓

Background

↓

Research Methodology

↓

Findings

↓

Analysis

↓

Insights

↓

Recommendations

↓

Risks

↓

Implementation Considerations

↓

Open Questions

↓

References

↓

Appendices (Optional)
```

---

# 1. Metadata

Purpose

Provide basic information about the document.

Recommended Fields

- Title
- Module
- Version
- Status
- Author
- Created Date
- Updated Date
- Review Cycle
- Related Modules
- Tags

This section helps with organization and version control.

---

# 2. Executive Summary

Purpose

Provide a concise overview.

Include

- Research objective
- Key findings
- Major recommendations
- Important risks

Target length

Approximately one page or less.

Decision-makers should understand the document by reading only this section.

---

# 3. Objective

Answer:

- Why was this research conducted?
- What decisions will it support?
- What outcome is expected?

Clearly define the purpose before presenting results.

---

# 4. Scope

Define what is included and excluded.

Include

- Topics covered
- Assumptions
- Constraints
- Limitations

Clear scope prevents misunderstanding.

---

# 5. Background

Provide context.

Explain

- Industry
- Business context
- Existing solutions
- Market conditions
- Relevant history

Readers unfamiliar with the topic should understand the context after reading this section.

---

# 6. Research Methodology

Describe how the research was conducted.

Include

- Research approach
- Information sources
- Techniques used
- Validation process
- Limitations

Transparency increases trust.

---

# 7. Findings

Present factual observations.

Include

- Market findings
- User findings
- Competitor findings
- Technical findings
- Business findings

Avoid recommendations in this section.

Present evidence objectively.

---

# 8. Analysis

Interpret the findings.

Examples

- SWOT Analysis
- Gap Analysis
- Trend Analysis
- Root Cause Analysis
- Opportunity Analysis

Focus on explaining why the findings matter.

---

# 9. Insights

Transform analysis into actionable understanding.

Each insight should answer:

- What was discovered?
- Why is it important?
- How does it affect the product?

Insights should connect evidence to decision-making.

---

# 10. Recommendations

Provide practical guidance.

Each recommendation should include:

- Description
- Reasoning
- Expected benefits
- Trade-offs
- Risks
- Priority
- Confidence level

Recommendations should always be justified.

---

# 11. Risks

Identify potential issues.

Document

- Business risks
- Technical risks
- Operational risks
- Legal risks
- User risks

Where appropriate, include mitigation strategies.

---

# 12. Implementation Considerations

Prepare for execution.

Include

- Dependencies
- Required resources
- Estimated complexity
- Technical considerations
- Organizational considerations
- Suggested implementation order

Research should help engineering begin confidently.

---

# 13. Open Questions

Research rarely answers everything.

List:

- Remaining uncertainties
- Validation needs
- Future investigations
- Pending decisions

Open questions should be visible rather than hidden.

---

# 14. References

Document all important sources.

Prefer:

1. Official documentation
2. Government publications
3. Academic research
4. Industry reports
5. Product documentation
6. Customer interviews
7. Community discussions

Provide enough information for others to verify the research.

---

# 15. Appendices (Optional)

Use appendices for supporting material.

Examples

- Interview notes
- Comparison tables
- Survey results
- Diagrams
- Technical references
- Glossary

Appendices should support the main document without interrupting its flow.

---

# Writing Guidelines

Research should be:

- Clear
- Concise
- Structured
- Objective
- Evidence-based
- Actionable

Avoid unnecessary repetition.

Every section should contribute value.

---

# Recommended Writing Style

Prefer:

- Short paragraphs
- Meaningful headings
- Bullet lists where appropriate
- Logical progression
- Consistent terminology

Avoid:

- Marketing language
- Unsupported claims
- Personal opinions
- Ambiguous wording
- Excessive jargon

Clarity is more valuable than complexity.

---

# Quality Expectations

Every document should be:

- Complete
- Accurate
- Traceable
- Reproducible
- Maintainable
- Implementation-ready

A document is complete only when another researcher can understand how every major conclusion was reached.

---

# Template Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- Metadata is complete.
- Objectives are clear.
- Scope is defined.
- Methodology is documented.
- Findings are evidence-based.
- Analysis is logical.
- Insights are meaningful.
- Recommendations are justified.
- Risks are documented.
- References are included.

# Self Assessment

Ask yourself:

- Would another researcher understand this document?
- Can every recommendation be traced back to evidence?
- Are important assumptions documented?
- Is the document ready for implementation?
- Does every section provide value?

If any answer is **No**, revise the document.
