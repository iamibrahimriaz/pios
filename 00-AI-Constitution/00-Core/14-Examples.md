# Examples

---

# Overview

Understanding principles is important.

Seeing them applied correctly is even more valuable.

This document contains examples of well-structured thinking rather than complete research documents.

The goal is to demonstrate **how Product Intelligence OS expects research to be performed**.

These examples should be used as references—not copied directly.

---

# Example 1 — Understanding the Problem

## ❌ Poor Example

```
We should build an AI chatbot for doctors because AI is popular.
```

Problems:

- Starts with a solution.
- No identified problem.
- No user research.
- No evidence.
- No business reasoning.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Doctors spend significant time writing repetitive prescriptions, reviewing patient history, and performing administrative tasks.

Research should determine:

- How much time is spent on these activities?
- Which tasks are repetitive?
- Which tasks create the most frustration?
- Which activities can be safely automated?

Only after understanding these problems should AI opportunities be evaluated.
```

Why it is better:

- Begins with the problem.
- Defines research objectives.
- Avoids assumptions.
- Leaves room for evidence.

---

# Example 2 — Recommendation

## ❌ Poor Example

```
Use React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Docker, and Kubernetes.
```

Problems:

- No justification.
- No alternatives.
- No business context.
- Technology-first thinking.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
The product requires:

- Fast web development
- Cross-platform compatibility
- Large developer ecosystem
- Long-term maintainability

React is recommended because it aligns with these requirements.

Alternative frameworks such as Vue and Angular should also be evaluated before making the final technology decision.
```

Why it is better:

- Explains reasoning.
- Connects technology to requirements.
- Considers alternatives.

---

# Example 3 — Competitor Research

## ❌ Poor Example

```
Competitor A has more features.
```

Problems:

- No evidence.
- No comparison.
- No insight.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Competitor A focuses on enterprise customers by offering extensive customization and advanced reporting.

Competitor B focuses on small businesses through a simpler interface and lower pricing.

This suggests an opportunity for a product that combines ease of use with selected enterprise capabilities.
```

Why it is better:

- Compares competitors.
- Generates insight.
- Identifies opportunity.

---

# Example 4 — User Research

## ❌ Poor Example

```
Users need a dashboard.
```

Problems:

- Assumption.
- No supporting evidence.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Interviews reveal that users struggle to locate critical information quickly.

Rather than assuming a dashboard is needed, research should identify:

- Which information users access most frequently.
- When they need it.
- Which decisions depend on it.

The interface should then be designed around those findings.
```

Why it is better:

- Research-driven.
- User-centered.
- Avoids unnecessary features.

---

# Example 5 — AI Recommendation

## ❌ Poor Example

```
Let's use AI everywhere.
```

Problems:

- Technology-driven.
- No measurable value.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Prescription writing contains repetitive tasks such as medicine lookup, dosage suggestions, and formatting.

These activities are suitable candidates for AI assistance because they:

- Save time.
- Reduce repetitive work.
- Improve consistency.

AI should assist the doctor, not replace clinical judgment.
```

Why it is better:

- Defines a real use case.
- Shows measurable value.
- Maintains appropriate human oversight.

---

# Example 6 — Risk Assessment

## ❌ Poor Example

```
There are no significant risks.
```

Problems:

- Unrealistic.
- Ignores uncertainty.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Potential risks include:

Business:
- Low adoption among target users.

Technical:
- Integration complexity with third-party systems.

Legal:
- Healthcare data privacy regulations.

Mitigation strategies should be planned before implementation.
```

Why it is better:

- Identifies multiple risk categories.
- Encourages proactive planning.

---

# Example 7 — Insight Generation

## ❌ Poor Example

```
The market is growing.
```

Problems:

- Only states a fact.
- No business value.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
The market is growing rapidly, but most competitors continue targeting large organizations.

This creates an opportunity to build a lightweight solution specifically for independent professionals who remain underserved.
```

Why it is better:

- Converts information into strategy.
- Identifies opportunity.

---

# Example 8 — Research Conclusion

## ❌ Poor Example

```
The product looks good.
```

Problems:

- Subjective.
- Unsupported.

---

## ✅ Good Example

```
Research indicates a validated market need, limited direct competition within the target segment, and a sustainable business model.

Remaining uncertainties include pricing validation and customer acquisition costs, which should be addressed during MVP testing.
```

Why it is better:

- Evidence-based.
- Balanced.
- Documents uncertainty.

# Common Characteristics of Good Research

High-quality research:

- Starts with questions.
- Defines objectives.
- Uses reliable evidence.
- Separates facts from assumptions.
- Explains reasoning.
- Evaluates alternatives.
- Identifies risks.
- Produces actionable recommendations.

---

# Common Characteristics of Poor Research

Poor research often:

- Begins with a preferred solution.
- Assumes user needs.
- Copies competitors.
- Ignores evidence.
- Recommends technology without justification.
- Omits risks.
- Provides vague conclusions.
- Lacks implementation guidance.

---

# How to Use These Examples

Use these examples to:

- Learn expected quality.
- Compare your work.
- Improve documentation.
- Review AI outputs.
- Train new contributors.

Do not copy them directly into research documents.

Every project requires original thinking.

---

# Self Assessment

Compare your research against these examples.

Ask:

- Does my work resemble the good examples?
- Have I avoided the mistakes shown in the poor examples?
- Can I explain why my recommendations are correct?
- Are my conclusions supported by evidence?

If not, continue improving the research.
