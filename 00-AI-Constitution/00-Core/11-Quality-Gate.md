# Quality Gate

---

# Overview

Completing research is not the same as completing **good research**.

Before any research can be accepted, it must pass a standardized Quality Gate.

The purpose of the Quality Gate is to ensure that every research project is:

- Complete
- Accurate
- Consistent
- Actionable
- Evidence-based
- Implementation-ready

Research that does not meet these standards should be revised before delivery.

---

# Quality Philosophy

Quality is measured by evidence, reasoning, and usefulness.

A document is not considered high quality because it is long.

It is considered high quality because it enables confident decision-making.

---

# Quality Gate Workflow

```
Research Completed

↓

Self Review

↓

Quality Validation

↓

Gap Identification

↓

Corrections

↓

Final Review

↓

Approved
```

No project should skip this workflow.

---

# Gate 1 — Completeness

Verify that all required research areas have been addressed.

Questions

- Is the problem clearly defined?
- Are users understood?
- Has the market been researched?
- Have competitors been analyzed?
- Has the business model been considered?
- Has the technical direction been explored?
- Have risks been documented?

Pass Criteria

No critical research area is missing.

---

# Gate 2 — Clarity

Research should be understandable.

Verify:

- Clear language
- Logical structure
- Well-defined terminology
- No ambiguous statements
- No contradictory conclusions

If another researcher cannot understand the document, it is not ready.

---

# Gate 3 — Evidence

Every important conclusion should be supported.

Verify:

- Reliable sources
- Multiple references where appropriate
- Clear distinction between facts and assumptions
- Traceable reasoning

Opinions without evidence should not pass.

---

# Gate 4 — Consistency

Research should be internally consistent.

Check for:

- Contradictions
- Conflicting recommendations
- Duplicate findings
- Inconsistent terminology
- Misaligned priorities

Consistency builds trust.

---

# Gate 5 — Reasoning

Recommendations should explain:

- Why this decision?
- Why not another option?
- What evidence supports it?
- What trade-offs exist?
- What risks remain?

Reasoning should be visible.

---

# Gate 6 — Actionability

Research should enable action.

Verify:

- Recommendations are specific.
- Priorities are clear.
- Next steps are defined.
- Decisions are implementable.

If readers do not know what to do next, the research is incomplete.

---

# Gate 7 — Feasibility

Recommendations should be realistic.

Evaluate:

- Technical feasibility
- Business feasibility
- Operational feasibility
- Financial feasibility
- Resource availability

Practical solutions create successful products.

---

# Gate 8 — Risk Assessment

Research should acknowledge uncertainty.

Verify:

- Technical risks
- Business risks
- Market risks
- Security risks
- Legal risks
- Assumptions

Every significant recommendation should include associated risks.

---

# Gate 9 — Documentation Quality

Documentation should be:

- Organized
- Complete
- Reusable
- Searchable
- Maintainable

Future contributors should easily understand the research.

---

# Gate 10 — Continuous Improvement

Every project should improve Product Intelligence OS.

Capture:

- Lessons learned
- Better questions
- Better methodologies
- Better templates
- Better workflows

Research is complete only after knowledge has been returned to the framework.

---

# Quality Indicators

High-quality research demonstrates:

- Clear objectives
- Thorough investigation
- Evidence-based conclusions
- Logical reasoning
- Balanced recommendations
- Transparent assumptions
- Practical implementation guidance

---

# Quality Indicators to Avoid

Poor-quality research often includes:

- Unsupported conclusions
- Excessive assumptions
- Weak evidence
- Missing context
- Incomplete analysis
- Generic recommendations
- Unclear priorities
- No risk assessment

These should trigger additional review.

---

# Approval Checklist

Before approving research, verify:

✓ Objectives achieved

✓ Questions answered

✓ Research completed

✓ Evidence verified

✓ Assumptions documented

✓ Alternatives evaluated

✓ Risks identified

✓ Recommendations justified

✓ Documentation organized

✓ Implementation ready

Only when every item passes should the project be approved.

---

# Confidence Assessment

Every major recommendation should include a confidence level.

Suggested scale:

| Confidence | Meaning                                                       |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Very High  | Strong evidence with multiple supporting sources.             |
| High       | Well-supported with minor uncertainty.                        |
| Medium     | Reasonable evidence but additional validation is recommended. |
| Low        | Limited evidence; treat as exploratory.                       |
| Unknown    | Insufficient information to make a reliable recommendation.   |

Confidence should reflect evidence, not optimism.

---

# Exit Criteria

Research passes the Quality Gate only when:

- Objectives are achieved.
- Evidence supports conclusions.
- Assumptions are explicit.
- Recommendations are actionable.
- Risks are documented.
- Documentation is implementation-ready.
- Quality review has been completed.

Otherwise, return to the appropriate research stage.

---

# Self Assessment

Before marking the project complete, ask:

- Would I trust these recommendations if I were investing my own time and resources?
- Can every important decision be explained?
- Can another researcher reproduce the reasoning?
- Are any critical unknowns hidden?
- Is the documentation ready for engineering?

If any answer is **No**, continue refining the research.
